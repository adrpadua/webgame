# Gazetteer Of The Warded Reaches

Status: active setting canon for geography, nations, settlements, travel logic, and encounter theming. This document does not create gameplay rules.

## Shape Of The Known World

The Warded Reaches are not one empire. They are a connected disaster belt: river deltas, volcanic marches, glass islands, relay mountains, salt flats, and canal-cities bound by oathcraft treaties because catastrophe does not respect borders.

Most maps place the **Meridian Rivers** at the center. To the east are furnace highlands and ember-coral vents. To the west are the Glasswake Isles. North rises the Relay Crown, where aether signals cross mountain sanctuaries. South stretches the Salt Quiet, where failed instruments sleep under white flats.

## Major Regions

### The Meridian Rivers

Type: Geography

Three wide red-silt rivers that feed the central floodplain and carry trade, refugees, signal barges, and oathcraft components. The rivers are treated almost as public persons: they have legal rights of flow, named moods, and annual appeasement maintenance.

Design use:

- river evacuation scenarios;
- gate-and-lock settlements;
- flood magic, signal cloth, civic oathcraft;
- Heroes from disciplined public-service cultures.

### Redwater Locks

Type: Settlement

Formerly the great canal-fortress city of the Meridian Rivers. It was built in terraces between floodwalls, canal locks, ward districts, and signal gates. Redwater Locks is now a ruined city and a diaspora identity.

Why so many gates:

- Tide gates held back red storm-surge.
- Canal locks raised barges between districts.
- Flood doors sealed low streets.
- Ward gates isolated disasters.
- Civic gates authenticated evacuation counts.
- Signal gates projected safe routes through smoke and panic.

Current state:

The eastern locks are destroyed. The upper district is intermittently accessible. Salvage crews enter only under escort because failed oath locks still obey old counts, damaged runeglass creates false corridors, and some gates open for names that are no longer living.

Story use:

- Elian's origin;
- tutorial ruins for gate mechanics;
- memory-heavy raid staging ground;
- future boss tied to oathcraft failure.

### The Furnace Marches

Type: Geography / Frontier Region

Black stone highlands veined with ember coral, heat organs, and furnace vents. Embermaw's material language comes from this region. The Marches are not empty; mining orders, heat-priests, coral cutters, and hazard surveyors live there under strict cooling protocols.

Story use:

- Embermaw and other heat bosses;
- fire-as-infrastructure, not generic fire magic;
- Whelps as splintered heat-life rather than baby dragons;
- tension between resource extraction and disaster awakening.

### The Glasswake Isles

Type: Geography / Maritime Culture

An island chain where beaches are made of translucent stormglass and ships navigate by reflected constellations. Glasswake people specialize in lenses, pattern projectors, and long-distance omen reading. They distrust locked gates and prefer open sightlines.

Story use:

- ranged damage Hero culture;
- optical mechanics, beams, reflections, false telegraphs;
- contrast with Redwater's closed-gate civic order.

### The Relay Crown

Type: Geography / Settlement Network

A ring of mountain sanctuaries and relay towers that transmit aether signals between cities. The Crown is politically neutral by treaty, though every power tries to influence it.

Story use:

- healer and support Hero origins;
- communication failures;
- relay-tower boss arenas;
- moral conflicts around who gets warning first.

### The Salt Quiet

Type: Geography / Forbidden Zone

A southern expanse of white salt flats where old boss instruments were buried after the Instrument Wars. The Quiet is not silent. It hums underfoot when storms approach.

Story use:

- late-game ancient instruments;
- forbidden salvage;
- bosses that feel engineered rather than biological;
- questions about whether the Quiet is a prison or an incubator.

## Political Powers

### The Meridian Concord

Type: Organization

A treaty network of river cities, canal settlements, and refugee wards. It values passage law, evacuation rights, and public recordkeeping. Redwater Locks was one of its proudest members.

Public face: disciplined, humane, procedural.

Shadow: slow to act when no protocol fits; willing to preserve records even when people need improvisation.

### The Veyr Aster Relay Houses

Type: Organization

Mountain families and technical orders that maintain aether relay towers. They claim neutrality, but neutrality is power when everyone depends on your warnings.

Public face: serene signal-keepers.

Shadow: decide which messages arrive first.

### The Kharuun Furnace League

Type: Organization

Mining cities and heat-engine guilds in the Furnace Marches. They harvest ember coral and understand furnace-bodied entities better than anyone, which makes everyone else suspicious of them.

Public face: necessary industrial specialists.

Shadow: accused of waking the things they profit from fighting.

### The Glasswake Compact

Type: Organization

Island lenswrights, navigators, duelists, and open-water settlements. They produce the best pattern projectors and the most independent raid scouts.

Public face: brilliant, stylish, impossible to corner.

Shadow: sell sight to all sides.

### The Nocturne Quorum

Type: Organization

Archivists of the Salt Quiet and custodians of forbidden instruments. They insist some knowledge must remain locked away, then ask to be trusted with every key.

Public face: solemn guardians of historical danger.

Shadow: may know more about the Ashwake than they admit.

## Travel Logic

Travel is shaped by safety protocols, not wilderness wandering.

- Between major cities, people travel by relay-barge, signal road, gate caravan, or sanctioned air-sail.
- Every route has warning colors, safe houses, and fallback shelters.
- Raid parties travel with permits because their equipment can trigger oath locks or panic protocols.
- A city without visible evacuation infrastructure is considered either poor, reckless, or hiding something.

## Everyday Life

The world should feel lived-in between raids:

- Children learn signal colors as nursery rhymes.
- Weddings include oath-lock witnesses so future children inherit right-of-passage.
- Markets sell gate charms that are mostly decorative but emotionally important.
- People argue about famous raid parties the way sports fans argue about teams.
- Public squares display evacuation maps as civic art.
- "Count the living" is a blessing, a command, and sometimes an accusation.
