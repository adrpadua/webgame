# World History Of The Warded Reaches

Status: active setting canon for fiction and art direction. This document does not create gameplay rules.

## Historical Frame

People in the Warded Reaches count history from the **First Binding**, the earliest surviving oathcraft event that all major calendars can agree upon. Dates are written as `FB`.

The current era is **719 FB**, an age of professional raid companies, civic evacuation protocols, and increasingly frequent boss awakenings.

## Era I: Before The First Binding

The earliest cultures remembered the world as a place of beautiful disasters. Rivers changed course overnight. Mountains rang like bells. Storms learned the names of villages. A settlement survived only if it could read patterns in catastrophe.

The oldest surviving myths agree on one thing: the world has never been inert. Stone remembers pressure. Rivers remember paths. Fire remembers hunger. Magic was not invented; it was negotiated.

### Key Legacies

- The first runeglass lenses were not weapons. They were flood mirrors used to show whether a river intended to rise.
- Early oath knots used cloth, witness marks, and repeated phrases to bind crews to dangerous communal labor.
- The first "Heroes" were not champions. They were people trusted to stand inside a pattern and finish a public task.

## Era II: The First Binding, 0-83 FB

The First Binding occurred when seven floodplain settlements linked their signal towers during a season of red storms. Instead of each town defending itself, they agreed to a shared rule: if one tower burned blue, every gate opened outward; if one tower burned red, every gate closed inward; if one tower burned white, every able watch crew moved toward the danger.

That shared protocol became the first oathcraft network.

### Why It Matters

Oathcraft proved that magic became stronger when it was public, material, and repeated. A private spell could save one person. A civic protocol could save a district.

### Major Developments

- **Oathsteel** emerged when smiths learned to quench metal in witnessed promises.
- **Living gold** became the preferred mechanism for locks because it could remember authorized command phrases.
- **Signal cloth** spread across river cities as a visible language for evacuation and battlefield roles.

## Era III: The Gatewright Centuries, 84-311 FB

After the First Binding, settlements competed to build better public magic. Gatewrights became architects, priests, engineers, and emergency planners at once. Cities no longer asked "How high is the wall?" They asked "How many ways can this place fail, and what opens when it does?"

The great canal cities were born in this era, including **Redwater Locks**.

### Redwater Locks Rises

Redwater Locks was built where three red-silt rivers met the sea. Its founders believed a city should not be a single fortress but a sequence of survivable compartments. Tide gates held back storm surge. Canal locks lifted barges through stepped districts. Ward gates separated neighborhoods into oathcraft cells. Signal gates turned streets into lit evacuation corridors.

Redwater's central doctrine became famous: **"No gate closes until the count is known."**

### Cultural Consequences

- Civic record-keepers became sacred professionals.
- Gate watches became more respected than noble cavalry in many river states.
- Public evacuation drills became seasonal festivals as much as emergency practice.
- Children learned gate signs before they learned formal script.

## Era IV: The Instrument Wars, 312-460 FB

Prosperity brought ambition. Gate engines, medicant rigs, pattern projectors, and aether relays were no longer used only for survival. Nations started using oathcraft as leverage: redirecting rivers, locking trade roads, sealing rivals out of harvest districts, and building weapons that could repeat a battle plan without human courage.

The worst of these weapons were called **boss instruments**. They were not monsters in the old sense. They were catastrophe bodies built around repeatable behavior.

### Major Conflicts

| Conflict | Years | Summary | Legacy |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| The Latch-Toll War | 337-344 FB | River princes charged passage through lifesaving gates during flood season. | Created the first international right-of-passage laws. |
| The Brass Orchard Revolt | 371-373 FB | Living-gold engines used to control farm labor began obeying local vowkeepers instead of governors. | Proved oathcraft could reject illegitimate authority. |
| The Black Relay Siege | 428-431 FB | Aether relays were used to transmit false evacuation orders. | Led to witness-key authentication and modern command protocols. |

## Era V: The Quiet Concord, 461-611 FB

After the Instrument Wars, the largest powers signed the **Quiet Concord**, a treaty that banned self-sustaining boss instruments and defined raid-scale threats as international emergencies. It did not end conflict, but it gave people a shared legal language for disaster.

This era created the first licensed raid companies. Their job was not exploration or treasure hunting. Their job was to answer threats too mobile, strange, or overwhelming for a single city.

### Institutions Founded

- **The Concordance of Gates:** treaty court for passage, evacuation, and shared disaster response.
- **The Witness Registry:** independent recordkeepers who certify vows, contracts, deaths, and rescue counts.
- **The Pattern College:** an academy for telegraph interpretation, boss-behavior study, and protocol design.
- **The Blue Lantern Fleet:** civilian evacuation barges that can be requisitioned by treaty.

## Era VI: The Ashwake, 612-701 FB

The Ashwake began when old boss instruments and natural apex entities started awakening in clusters. Some blamed treaty violations. Some blamed deep-earth heat cycles. Some blamed broken divine machinery below the world. No theory has won.

Raid companies became famous, controversial, and necessary. Children collected sigil cards of tank captains and storm duelists. Governments funded Heroes and feared them. Ordinary people learned to judge a city by its evacuation routes.

### The Fall Of Redwater Locks, 698 FB

Redwater Locks fell during a disaster still known only as **the Eastern Breach**. The floodwall failed first. Then the outer ward gates misread command phrases. Then fire moved through the lower lockhouses faster than runners could carry orders.

Captain Elian Voss disobeyed the retreat order and held a broken lock corridor long enough for the last evacuation count to cross the river. Redwater Locks was lost. Its people survived in barges, relay sanctuaries, and host cities across the Reaches.

Survivors began calling Elian **The Last Gate**.

## Era VII: The Current Clock, 702-719 FB

The present is tense, bright, and overbuilt. Cities have beautiful armor over old fear. Raid companies are professionalized, sponsored, argued over, and mythologized. The Quiet Concord still stands, but every new boss awakening makes people wonder whether the treaty solved anything or merely bought time.

**Embermaw** belongs to this era: a furnace-bodied boss whose heat patterns, Whelp pressure, and lane denial look natural until studied closely.

## Current Historical Questions

- Who or what is teaching disasters to break civic oathcraft?
- Was Redwater Locks a one-off tragedy or a rehearsal?
- Are raid bosses awakening, being manufactured, or being called?
- Can a party of Heroes prevent a catastrophe without becoming a new kind of boss instrument?
- What happens when public magic depends on vows but public trust begins to fail?
