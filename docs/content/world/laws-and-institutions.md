# Laws And Institutions Of The Warded Reaches

Status: active setting canon for legal flavor, civic institutions, raid-company politics, and story hooks. This document does not create gameplay rules.

## Legal Premise

Law in the Warded Reaches grew from disaster. The oldest legal question was not "Who owns this land?" but "Who opens the gate when the river rises?"

Modern law is built around four public duties:

1. **Passage:** civilians must be able to move away from catastrophe.
2. **Count:** the living, missing, dead, and evacuated must be recorded.
3. **Witness:** vows and emergency orders must be verifiable.
4. **Answer:** raid-scale threats must be studied and opposed before they become territorial facts.

## The Quiet Concord

Type: Treaty

The Quiet Concord ended the worst years of the Instrument Wars and remains the legal spine of international disaster response.

Core clauses:

- Self-sustaining boss instruments are forbidden.
- Civilian evacuation routes cannot be taxed during active disaster.
- Blue Lantern passage is neutral and protected.
- Raid companies require public registration or emergency witness.
- Pattern research on active boss behavior must be shared with threatened neighbors.
- Buried instruments in the Salt Quiet cannot be excavated without Concordance approval.

Political tension:

Every power claims to obey the Quiet Concord. Every power also keeps exceptions, secret studies, old prototypes, or "defensive" instruments.

## The Concordance Of Gates

Type: Court / International Organization

The Concordance of Gates judges disputes between cities, nations, guilds, and raid companies. Its courts are built with no single front door. Petitioners enter through different gates based on the kind of claim they bring: passage, count, witness, oath breach, instrument crime, or raid liability.

Common cases:

- a city closed a gate before the civic count was complete;
- a raid company caused collateral damage while answering a boss;
- a noble house forged an oath-lock command phrase;
- an organization hoarded pattern data;
- a Blue Lantern vessel was delayed, attacked, or impersonated.

Story use:

- political trial episodes;
- raid-company licensing drama;
- moral conflicts after a "successful" fight still harms civilians;
- courtroom scenes where architecture visibly encodes law.

## The Witness Registry

Type: Civic Order / Recordkeeping Institution

Witness clerks certify vows, deaths, rescue counts, passage rights, contracts, and disaster records. They are trained to be calm, portable, and irritatingly precise.

What they carry:

- wax tablets for immediate counts;
- runeglass seals for official copies;
- signal cloth strips color-coded by emergency type;
- small living-gold witness locks that close only when a record is accepted by all required parties.

Public reputation:

Loved by survivors who need names preserved. Hated by commanders who want ambiguity.

## The Pattern College

Type: Academy / Research Organization

The Pattern College studies boss behavior, encounter telegraphs, protocol design, and failure analysis. Its students learn to describe terror in useful language.

Departments:

- **Telegraphics:** projected danger, safe lanes, pattern repetition.
- **Catastrophe Anatomy:** furnace organs, oath engines, living weapons, corrupted divine bodies.
- **Role Protocols:** Tank, Healer, Damage, support, and future party responsibility theory.
- **Failure Studies:** disasters where oathcraft did not behave as expected.

Reputation:

Brilliant after the fact. Uneasy before the fact.

## Raid Company Law

Type: Profession / Legal Framework

Raid companies are licensed because their work crosses borders, risks civilians, and involves technologies that can trigger civic protocols.

Requirements:

- at least one registered tactical lead;
- named role responsibilities;
- witnessable after-action records;
- oathcraft inspection for dangerous equipment;
- disaster liability bond unless operating under emergency call.

Emergency exception:

If a raid-scale threat is active and delay would likely cost civilian lives, an unregistered party can act under **field witness**. This saves lives and creates legal messes later.

## Passage Crimes

Type: Law

Passage crimes are treated more severely than ordinary property crimes because they attack survival infrastructure.

Examples:

- closing a civic gate before count transfer;
- falsifying a safe route;
- impersonating a Blue Lantern vessel;
- charging passage toll during active disaster;
- corrupting signal cloth or aether relay output;
- using a ward gate to trap a district for political gain.

Punishments:

Punishment often includes public service in dangerous infrastructure rather than imprisonment. The law's logic is blunt: if you abused passage, you will learn what passage costs.

## Oathcraft Crimes

Type: Law / Magic

Oathcraft crimes corrupt the trust that makes protocol magic function.

Examples:

- forging a command phrase;
- binding a vow without informed witness;
- modifying a living-gold lock to obey bloodline over civic duty;
- hiding failed pattern data;
- waking or repairing a banned boss instrument;
- using a medicant rig to falsify death, injury, or consent records.

Story use:

Oathcraft crimes are good long-term mysteries because the damage can look like "magic failed" until someone proves it was made to fail.

## Civilian Rights During Raid Events

Type: Law / Tradition

The Quiet Concord defines a raid event as a disaster with repeatable hostile behavior that exceeds ordinary civic defense.

Civilian rights:

- right to warning if warning is technically possible;
- right to Blue Lantern passage;
- right to truthful safe-route markings;
- right to have missing status recorded;
- right to refuse non-emergency oath binding;
- right to post-raid damage review.

Hard truth:

These rights are unevenly honored. Wealthy districts get better warnings. Poor districts know which gates stick.

## Titles

### Gate Captain

The commander responsible for a gate, lock district, or temporary evacuation line. Elian Voss held this role at Redwater Locks.

### Pattern Advocate

A lawyer-scholar trained to argue whether a raid company's interpretation of a boss pattern was reasonable.

### Lantern Marshal

A Blue Lantern Fleet officer with authority to requisition civilian boats during active disaster.

### Quiet Custodian

A Nocturne Quorum official responsible for sealed boss-instrument sites in the Salt Quiet.

## Story Questions

- What happens when a legal gate must stay open but a tactical Tank needs it closed?
- Can a raid company be guilty if they saved the city but broke the treaty?
- Who benefits when a civic count goes missing?
- What does a society do when its magic depends on public trust and public trust is politically expensive?
- Could Redwater Locks have fallen because someone followed the law too perfectly?
