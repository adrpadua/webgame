| 2026-08-17 | D-015 | Adopted | Riposte Ready is cashed by any Boss-damage card for `+1`; a legal Shield Slam takes the full `+2`. Cards without Boss damage never consume it. User-approved; options and rationale in [riposte-payoff-deepening.md](design-proposals/riposte-payoff-deepening.md). | Controlled-cohort evidence showed the earned window converting in only one of three seeds because Shield Slam (2 of 20 cards) was its only spender; an earned proc that silently evaporates is the worst payoff failure mode. The graded cash-out keeps Shield Slam the ceiling while making the floor never-zero and adding spend-order mastery. | Riposte probe off-payoff coverage, web engine tests, and a fresh evaluation cohort under the new rule — prior cohorts are historical evidence for the old rule only. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-016 | Adopted | The raid is a team game: a solo Hero defeating a Boss is a tuning defect signal, never a success criterion. Evaluation cohorts record any solo Boss kill as a red-flag finding. | User-stated design principle. The solo slice tests role execution to the Round-4 checkpoint; Boss defeat belongs to a Party. An economy or deck that solo-kills is over-rich or under-pressured by definition. | The deck-evaluation rubric's solo-slice boundary carries the rule; future Party-scale criteria supersede the solo framing, not the principle. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-017 | Adopted | Counter-pressure against Boss-range camping ships as authored content: Raking Claw carries a `+3` `unguarded_bonus` when no Hero holds the Guarded Front at resolution, and Embermaw's solo-slice health rises from `36` to `48` so the solo damage race also fails. `boss_damage` stays positionless per the 2026-08-16 ruling. | User directed the fix; issue 15 left the mechanism open. The unguarded bonus makes holding the Guarded Front the cheapest place to take the unavoidable hit (Warden identity), and the health raise closes the second dominant line the red-flag search found (a dodge-positioned double-attack racer). Both Tank Design Principle walls now hold: the 12-policy sweep shows 0% solo victory and 0% enrage survival, and the committed `embermaw_solo_ceiling` Scenario dies in Round 6 with Embermaw at 10 health. | Sweep metrics stay at 0% victory; the Scenario generator exits red-flag on any found solo victory; `boss_beats`, `content`, `parity`, `records`, and riposte probes cover the new field and values. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-018 | Adopted | The user reaffirmed ADR 0019: all rules, content, and UI work happens in the web Encounter Engine/Workbench only. The Godot codebase, `.tres` resources, and probe suite are frozen reference material; the D-015/D-017 Godot-side syncs were the last, kept as pushed rather than reverted. New-work gates are `npm test`, `npm run evaluate`, and the Scenario generator's D-016 red flag. `data/*.json` is the authored content source of truth (ADR 0020). | Dual maintenance made every rules change land twice and risks divergence; ADR 0019 already decided the platform and the merge-era work under-weighted it. | Live rules/content docs no longer point work at Godot artifacts; the probe-harness doc carries the freeze banner. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-019 | Adopted | Fortify grants a `Fortified` commitment that lands its 6 Armor at the NEXT Round start, after the Armor wipe. Card text: `Gain 6 Armor at the start of the next Round.` | Under the old rule, Slow-window Armor was wiped before any damage could reach it — Fortify was a functionally dead card in the live deck (the first-turn doc had already worked around it). The delayed grant realizes the card's stated intent (prepare for a future threat) and gives the Slow Window a unique job: Fortify is now the only card that can pre-block the next Round's Instant Row, including earning Riposte Ready from a fully blocked Instant Tank Hit. | Engine test covers the full arc: no immediate Armor, grant after the wipe, Instant-row block at zero Health loss, normal expiry a Round later. Deck-evaluation freshness: prior cohorts are historical evidence for the old rule. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-020 | Adopted | The boss-design direction is canon, documented as the [Encounter Design Bible](../rules/encounter-design-bible.md): the Boss is a generator of shared escalating problems; the Timeline is a core system, not UI; Tank/Healer/Damage are three questions about the same problem ("Tank owns mitigation, Healer owns sustain"); cooperation is preferentially structural ("no stat total occupies two hexes") over role-locked; escalation grows demand density, not just numbers. | User-supplied design handoff, converging with the tank/healer research and D-016/D-017. Refines the Role-Load-Bearing Beats rule: structurally-suited problems beat arbitrary role locks. | Party-scale playtests use the north-star question: can each player name their part and someone else's in the same sentence about the outcome? |
