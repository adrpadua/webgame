| 2026-08-17 | D-022 | Adopted | No rules-relevant random choice may resolve after the player window that answers it ([ADR 0025](../adr/0025-randomize-before-the-window-that-answers-it.md)). | ADR 0012 made randomness reproducible but never said *when* it may resolve, so a post-commitment roll would satisfy it while producing the research's named unfair-feeling failure. | 2026-08-17 | D-023 | Adopted | Escalation is the encounter's only clock ([ADR 0027](../adr/0027-make-escalation-the-encounters-single-clock.md)): one fixed `0`–`5` scale on every Boss, `+1` automatically each Round end, plus authored per-Beat acceleration for unanswered demands, with the top threshold as the hard wipe. `End-of-Clock Behavior` becomes that threshold's effect; `Enrage Timer` retires. Adopted in the solo Embermaw slice now. | A binary Round limit is a clock the party reads but never feels, and gives Damage nothing structural to own. More importantly it converts D-016's stalemate wall from a health value that better play erodes into an economic impossibility optimization cannot repair. The fixed scale is a literacy requirement; per-Beat acceleration keeps Boss identity from flattening into one attrition formula. | The twelve-policy sweep and regenerated Scenarios must still show `0%` solo victory and `0%` enrage survival. Prior cohorts become historical evidence for the old rule, per the D-015/D-019 pattern. This is the only law in the pass that changes live behavior; the sweep runs as a separate follow-up. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-024 | Adopted | Encounters are authored in three layers: **Boss Core** (the learnable Boss Program spine), **Encounter Module** (one `Module Slot` per Program, filled by one of several authored Beat groups, chosen by a printed shareable `Raid Seed`), and **Difficulty Layer** (must change a mechanic or requirement; numbers alone are not a layer). `Practice Mode` is deliberately not a Difficulty Layer: economic tolerance only, never a softened failure condition. Vocabulary adopted now; no modules authored until the Party model exists. | Pure scripts go rote and fully shuffled decks stop mastery transferring; a bounded slot keeps both. Slot granularity beats per-Beat parameter randomization, which is the shape that tempts an author into violating D-022, and keeps validation to N combinations. Excluding Practice Mode from the layer vocabulary is what stops "easier numbers" re-entering through the door the layer test closes. | First Party session. Modules are validated in combination, never independently — module overlap, not individual values, is where difficulty comes from. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-025 | Adopted | Role identity is superiority, not exclusivity. A problem is **run-ending** if one unanswered Round causes unavoidable defeat, permanent Hero loss, or an Escalation Threshold crossing; run-ending problems must carry at least one off-role answer at a premium, and elsewhere it is optional. Off-role costs draw from a fixed vocabulary: extra actions, a card discard, a Class Resource, or accepting partial damage. | Hard locks turn an encounter into matching coloured keys to coloured locks and let one exhausted player convert every mistake into deterministic failure. Requiring off-role lines everywhere would triple authoring cost on Beats where nobody would pay the premium, so the requirement is scoped to where a missing specialist would otherwise make failure automatic. A fixed cost vocabulary is learnable; one standardized currency is not universally payable. | First Party session, with the no-healer-clear test and role counterfactual value (D-027) as the reads. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-026 | Adopted | Committed destinations and selected abilities stay visible to the Party; face-down simultaneous commitment is rejected. Quarterbacking is designed out structurally, and decision concentration is the accountability. | Hidden commitment would break Committed Movement's collision rule and cost the shared-planning feel that makes the Timeline a party object rather than four private ones. The research itself concedes systemic ownership beats silence rules: when an answer needs more actions than one player controls, no one player can drive the fight. | Decision concentration (D-027), via the policy-sweep proxy now and the human instrument once Parties play. If one player proposes nearly every response, the fix is more demand density — never a rule forbidding conversation. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-027 | Adopted | Four encounter metrics are required, each falsifying one adopted law: Timeline conversion rate (D-021), failure attribution (D-022), decision concentration (D-026), and role counterfactual value (D-025). They join the automated solo-victory and enrage-survival gates. Attempts-to-first-clear and wipe-cause distribution become required once repeat-clear content exists; the remaining research metrics stay opt-in. | Repeatability and fairness are a bundle, not a single win rate. Four metrics with a named law behind each will actually get collected; eleven will not. The two deferred metrics need multiple attempts by the same group, which the solo slice cannot produce. | Definitions land in the [deck-evaluation rubric](deck-evaluation-rubric.md); the decision-concentration proxy lands in the [measurement plan](../artifacts/deck-evaluation-measurement-plan.md). Each metric is collected at the first session that can produce it. |
