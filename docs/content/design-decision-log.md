| 2026-08-17 | D-024 | Adopted | Encounters are authored in three layers: **Boss Core** (the learnable Boss Program spine), **Encounter Module** (one `Module Slot` per Program, filled by one of several authored Beat groups, chosen by a printed shareable `Raid Seed`), and **Difficulty Layer** (must change a mechanic or requirement; numbers alone are not a layer). `Practice Mode` is deliberately not a Difficulty Layer: economic tolerance only, never a softened failure condition. Rejected 2026-08-17: revision 2's content-source split of the Timeline rows (boss choreography above encounter pressure). | 2026-08-17 | D-025 | Adopted | Role identity is superiority, not exclusivity. A problem is **run-ending** if one unanswered Round causes unavoidable defeat, permanent Hero loss, or an Escalation Threshold crossing; run-ending problems must carry at least one off-role answer at a premium, and elsewhere it is optional. Off-role costs draw from a fixed vocabulary: extra actions, a card discard, a Class Resource, or accepting partial damage. | Hard locks turn an encounter into matching coloured keys to coloured locks and let one exhausted player convert every mistake into deterministic failure. Requiring off-role lines everywhere would triple authoring cost on Beats where nobody would pay the premium, so the requirement is scoped to where a missing specialist would otherwise make failure automatic. A fixed cost vocabulary is learnable; one standardized currency is not universally payable. | First Party session, with the no-healer-clear test and role counterfactual value (D-027) as the reads. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-026 | Adopted | Committed destinations and selected abilities stay visible to the Party; face-down simultaneous commitment is rejected. Quarterbacking is designed out structurally, and decision concentration is the accountability. | Hidden commitment would break Committed Movement's collision rule and cost the shared-planning feel that makes the Timeline a party object rather than four private ones. The research itself concedes systemic ownership beats silence rules: when an answer needs more actions than one player controls, no one player can drive the fight. Rejected 2026-08-17: revision 2's Team-Up cards, which let a player act during another's turn. We have no separate turns — Quick and Slow are simultaneous windows with public commitments, so Team-Up solves a sequential-turn problem we do not have. The one genuinely missing piece, committing conditional on an ally's committed action, is D-028's attachment primitive aimed at an ally. | Decision concentration (D-027), via the policy-sweep proxy now and the human instrument once Parties play. If one player proposes nearly every response, the fix is more demand density — never a rule forbidding conversation. |
