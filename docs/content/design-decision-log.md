| 2026-08-17 | D-023 | Adopted | Escalation is the encounter's only clock ([ADR 0027](../adr/0027-make-escalation-the-encounters-single-clock.md)): one fixed `0`–`5` scale on every Boss, `+1` automatically each Round end, plus authored per-Beat acceleration for unanswered demands, with the top threshold as the hard wipe. `End-of-Clock Behavior` becomes that threshold's effect; `Enrage Timer` retires. The fixed scale is a literacy requirement; per-Beat acceleration keeps Boss identity from flattening into one attrition formula. Shipped 2026-08-17 with two narrowings found in build: automatic ticks start at `roundLimit - 4` so ticks alone reach the top exactly at the Encounter Clock (a 0–5 scale ticking from Round 1 would be a five-Round clock), and acceleration ignores a Minion that arrived this Round because it spawns in the Incoming Row where no player window can reach it. | Sweep and Scenarios regenerated; prior cohorts are historical evidence for the old rule. `victory%` stays `0%` and `checkpoint%` is identical to the pre-change baseline on all twelve existing policies — the late start's purpose. Figures are dated observations — see ADR 0027's 2026-08-17 correction for why the originally recorded percentages went stale. Embermaw's Brood Call penalty is authored at `0` until a Whelp answer exists (D-003); the mechanism is covered against a priced catalog variant. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-024 | Adopted | Encounters are authored in three layers: **Boss Core** (the learnable Boss Program spine), **Encounter Module** (one `Module Slot` per Program, filled by one of several authored Beat groups, chosen by a printed shareable `Raid Seed`), and **Difficulty Layer** (must change a mechanic or requirement; numbers alone are not a layer). `Practice Mode` is deliberately not a Difficulty Layer: economic tolerance only, never a softened failure condition. Vocabulary adopted now; no modules authored until the Party model exists. | Pure scripts go rote and fully shuffled decks stop mastery transferring; a bounded slot keeps both. Slot granularity beats per-Beat parameter randomization, which is the shape that tempts an author into violating D-022, and keeps validation to N combinations. Excluding Practice Mode from the layer vocabulary is what stops "easier numbers" re-entering through the door the layer test closes. Rejected 2026-08-17: revision 2's content-source split of the Timeline rows (boss choreography above encounter pressure). The valuable half of it is a stability contract, which Boss Core and Module Slot already hold on the better axis — authoring stability rather than screen position — and the split does not survive contact with our content, since choreography and pressure share one authored program today. | First Party session. Modules are validated in combination, never independently — module overlap, not individual values, is where difficulty comes from. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-025 | Adopted | Role identity is superiority, not exclusivity. A problem is **run-ending** if one unanswered Round causes unavoidable defeat, permanent Hero loss, or an Escalation Threshold crossing; run-ending problems must carry at least one off-role answer at a premium, and elsewhere it is optional. Off-role costs draw from a fixed vocabulary: extra actions, a card discard, a Class Resource, or accepting partial damage. | Hard locks turn an encounter into matching coloured keys to coloured locks and let one exhausted player convert every mistake into deterministic failure. Requiring off-role lines everywhere would triple authoring cost on Beats where nobody would pay the premium, so the requirement is scoped to where a missing specialist would otherwise make failure automatic. A fixed cost vocabulary is learnable; one standardized currency is not universally payable. | First Party session, with the no-healer-clear test and role counterfactual value (D-027) as the reads. |
| 2026-08-17 | D-026 | Adopted | Committed destinations and selected abilities stay visible to the Party; face-down simultaneous commitment is rejected. Quarterbacking is designed out structurally, and decision concentration is the accountability. | Hidden commitment would break Committed Movement's collision rule and cost the shared-planning feel that makes the Timeline a party object rather than four private ones. The research itself concedes systemic ownership beats silence rules: when an answer needs more actions than one player controls, no one player can drive the fight. Rejected 2026-08-17: revision 2's Team-Up cards, which let a player act during another's turn. We have no separate turns — Quick and Slow are simultaneous windows with public commitments, so Team-Up solves a sequential-turn problem we do not have. The one genuinely missing piece, committing conditional on an ally's committed action, is D-028's attachment primitive aimed at an ally. | Decision concentration (D-027), via the policy-sweep proxy now and the human instrument once Parties play. If one player proposes nearly every response, the fix is more demand density — never a rule forbidding conversation. |

| 2026-08-17 | D-032 | Adopted | Attaching splits into two concepts by lifetime anchor, with no umbrella term.
