# World Style Bible

Status: active design grounding. This document guides visual design, character fiction, card naming, art direction, and encounter theming. It does not create executable rules; `EncounterEngine` remains authoritative.

## Creative North Star

The world is **super-high fantasy raid technology**: a setting where mythic materials, ceremonial engineering, and visible magic systems produce characters who read clearly in a mobile tactics game.

Reference blend:

- **Valorant:** clean silhouettes, strong identity props, readable color accents, and confident character branding.
- **League of Legends:** overdesigned hero kits, faction-coded materials, iconic weapons, and expressive fantasy exaggeration.
- **FFXIV raids:** role responsibility, choreographed boss mechanics, telegraphs, phase language, and spectacular but readable attack presentation.

Avoid:

- DnD tavern fantasy, mundane chainmail, generic medieval villages, and practical historical arms.
- Gritty low fantasy where everything is leather, mud, and iron.
- Vague spell glow with no material source or rule.
- Busy card art that hides the Hero silhouette, target pattern, or role fantasy.

## World Premise

Civilizations survive raid-scale threats by building **oathcraft**: magical infrastructure that binds intent, geometry, and material into repeatable battlefield protocols. A Hero is not merely someone with talent. A Hero is someone whose equipment, training, and personal vow can interface with these protocols under pressure.

Bosses are not random monsters. They are apex entities, corrupted engines, ancient living weapons, or mythic disasters whose behavior can be studied, predicted, and counterplayed. A raid is a planned confrontation between player-side oathcraft and boss-side catastrophe.

## Material Language

Materials should feel designed, symbolic, and readable at small scale.

| Material | Visual read | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oathsteel** | Dark metal with luminous inlay seams and hard geometric edges. | Shields, weapons, armor frames, action-bar ability art. |
| **Signal cloth** | Saturated cloth panels with faction marks, route lines, or warning trims. | Capes, banners, waist cloth, healer ribbons, role accents. |
| **Runeglass** | Translucent hard-light crystal panes with internal glyph grids. | Barriers, lenses, spell foci, telegraph projectors. |
| **Aether ceramic** | Smooth pale plates with colored cracks or embedded channels. | High-status armor, medical tech, ritual consoles. |
| **Ember coral** | Organic red-orange mineral growth with heat veins. | Embermaw hazards, fire bosses, corrupted terrain. |
| **Void basalt** | Matte black stone that eats rim light and shows thin star-like fractures. | Late-game bosses, arenas, unstable seals. |
| **Living gold** | Muted brass-gold that forms hinges, locks, and filigree mechanisms. | Noble engines, oath locks, gate mechanisms. |

Materials should imply function. If an effect blocks damage, something locks, layers, braces, unfolds, or projects. If an effect heals, something restores structure, reconnects flow, or stabilizes a pattern. If an effect deals damage, something focuses force through a named implement or geometry.

## Technology Level

The world has no mundane modern industry, firearms, radios, or screens. It does have magical equivalents when they are expressed through authored materials:

- **Aether relays** carry battlefield signals through runeglass and signal cloth.
- **Oath locks** authenticate vows, roles, and safe passages.
- **Pattern projectors** paint legal danger zones, safe lanes, and ritual geometry.
- **Gate engines** move people, seal breaches, and shape defensive space.
- **Medicant rigs** stabilize bodies with aether ceramic braces and restorative glyphs.
- **Boss instruments** can be ancient, biological, divine, or engineered, but their mechanics must be visible enough for counterplay.

Technology should feel ritualized and physical. Buttons, panels, and interfaces exist as levers, sigils, lenses, locks, rotating rings, animated glyph plates, or projected hex diagrams rather than sci-fi tablets.

## Magic System

Magic is **protocol magic**. It is strongest when a caster, tool, vow, and pattern align.

Core principles:

1. **Intent needs a vessel.** A spell should come from a weapon, crest, gauntlet, shield, focus, arena structure, or visible body feature.
2. **Geometry matters.** Cones, lines, rings, zones, and adjacency are not UI conveniences; they are how magic expresses force.
3. **Vows bind behavior.** Hero kits should reflect a declared promise: hold the line, mend the broken, hunt the marked, burn the opening, or command the storm.
4. **Costs are concrete.** Movement costs footing and options. Protection costs position. Burst costs setup. Healing costs timing.
5. **Magic leaves evidence.** Armor plates, scorch marks, cracked runeglass, tether lines, lingering glyphs, and status icons should explain what happened.

Avoid unlimited hand-wave magic. If a card effect cannot be explained by the Hero's materials, training, or vow, it probably belongs to a different Hero.

## Faction And Hero Design

Every Hero should have:

- one strong silhouette readable at phone size;
- one identity prop visible in every card illustration;
- two to four material signatures;
- one role color accent;
- one repeated shape language;
- one combat vow that justifies their kit.

Hero designs may be elegant, sharp, impossible, and overbuilt. Armor can float, unfold, lock, and project hard-light geometry. Weapons can be symbolic and oversized. However, each exaggeration must clarify role or gameplay.

Do not make Heroes look like generic RPG classes. A Tank is not "person in armor"; a Tank is a branded defensive system. A Healer is not "person with a staff"; a Healer is a recovery protocol with a body and a point of view. A Damage Hero is not "person with a sword"; a Damage Hero is a specialized engine for creating or exploiting burst windows.

## Elian Voss Style Anchor

Elian Voss should be redesigned through this lens without losing the Shield Wall mechanics.

Core upgrade:

- Redwater becomes **Redwater Locks**, a mythic canal-fortress city built around gate engines, flood control, transit locks, ward doors, and evacuation oathcraft.
- Elian's shield identity is a folding Gate Rig with oathsteel ribs, restrained living-gold lockwork, and runeglass barrier panes rather than a conventional tower shield.
- Armor effects look like gate plates sliding into place around Elian, not generic blue shielding.
- Riposte Ready looks like a lock releasing after a perfect block: the gate catches the boss's force, turns, and opens a narrow counter-strike.
- Interception projects a straight "safe passage" line from Elian to an ally, then pulls the hit onto the shield gate.

Recommended Elian material set:

- oathsteel frame;
- living-gold locks and hinges;
- weathered blue signal cloth;
- runeglass barrier panes;
- ember-red cord from Redwater Locks.

Elian's voice remains calm and spare, but their presentation can be more iconic: a quiet operator of an impossible fortress mechanism, not a realistic gate guard.

## Card Art Rules

Every card illustration should answer these questions at a glance:

- Which Hero owns this card?
- Which role job does this card express?
- What material or implement creates the effect?
- What board relationship matters: target, line, cone, adjacency, zone, or self?
- Is this setup, conversion, payoff, recovery, or emergency response?

Card art should prefer:

- bold Hero silhouettes;
- clear action poses;
- clean background geometry;
- visible material effects;
- one dominant visual idea.

Card art should avoid:

- generic portraits with no action;
- spell effects floating with no source;
- muddy battle scenes;
- too many small figures;
- low-contrast particle clouds;
- visual jokes or meme-like props.

## Encounter And Boss Style

Bosses should feel like authored raid spectacles, not wandering monsters.

Each Boss needs:

- a readable silhouette and material signature;
- two or three named mechanic families;
- visible telegraph language;
- at least one role-specific pressure;
- one phase escalation that combines previously learned rules.

Embermaw's current lane:

- ember coral, blackened oathsteel fragments, and furnace-like organs;
- attacks feel like controlled heat pressure, lane denial, and clutching claws;
- Whelps should look like splintered furnace sparks or minor heat-beasts, not generic baby dragons;
- Cinder Breath and Scorched terrain should read as arena-shaping heat geometry.

## Naming Rules

Names should feel like branded techniques, not generic fantasy spells.

Prefer:

- `Gate Lock`
- `Portcullis Break`
- `Bastion Array`
- `Oathline`
- `Last Lantern`
- `Furnace Cleave`
- `Cinder Pattern`
- `Molten Tail`

Avoid:

- `Magic Shield`
- `Fireball`
- `Power Strike`
- `Heal`
- `Taunt`
- `Holy Blast`, unless a future Hero's whole material and vow language supports holy spectacle.

Mechanically plain cards may have plain names when clarity matters, but final art/name passes should pull them toward the Hero's material system.

## UI And Readability Implications

[oathcraft-interface-direction.md](oathcraft-interface-direction.md) applies this section to concrete materials, colour values, and component anatomy for card frames, controls, panels, and gauges. The implications below remain the grounding; that document is how they are built.

The HUD should present this world as readable raid technology:

- telegraphs are projected encounter geometry, not flat debug shapes;
- status icons look like compact material symbols;
- health and Armor bars should feel integrated with Hero frames;
- card inspection should show full branded art, while compact cards preserve name, timing, Charge Value, and role-relevant iconography;
- color coding should support roles and mechanics, not become a one-note palette.

Mobile readability beats decorative detail. The fantasy is overdesigned in silhouette and material logic, but the tactical state stays clean.

## Design Review Checklist

Before approving a new Hero, Boss, card family, or art direction, ask:

- Does it avoid generic DnD fantasy?
- Can a player describe its material source?
- Does the magic obey a visible vessel, geometry, vow, or cost?
- Does the silhouette read at phone size?
- Does the design reinforce a role responsibility?
- Does the card or mechanic look like it belongs to this world and not a different game?
- Would the effect still be readable during a fast raid turn?
