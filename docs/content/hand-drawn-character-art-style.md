# Hand-Drawn Character Art Style

Status: active art-direction recipe. Use this with [world-style-bible.md](world-style-bible.md) when generating or briefing Hero concept art, card art, and visual explorations. This document is visual guidance only; it does not change rules or content behavior.

## Purpose

The current target style is **clean hand-drawn super-high fantasy character concept art**. It should keep the world bible's myth-tech materials and raid-role identity, but avoid the overly busy rendered look that came from dense layered plates, photoreal lighting, and too many mechanical parts.

The goal is not to make the world less advanced. The goal is to make advanced armor and magic readable through simplified shapes, strong silhouettes, and limited visual ideas.

## Relationship To The Shipping Art

This document is the committed target for the game's real art. Everything currently rendered is a placeholder.

The live skin is CC0 art borrowed from OpenDuelyst — battlefield backdrop, board and hover tiles, target tile, and neutral boss crest — catalogued in [assets/README.md](../../assets/README.md) and described in [embermaw-vertical-slice.md](../artifacts/embermaw-vertical-slice.md). It was chosen for legibility while the tactical surface was being built, not as art direction. It does not follow the material language in [world-style-bible.md](world-style-bible.md), and it should not be used as a reference when briefing new work.

Two consequences worth holding onto:

- New art is briefed against this document and the world bible, never against what is on screen today.
- Replacing the placeholders is a known outstanding job, not a regression to be discovered later. Board, backdrop, tiles, crest, and Hero art all come from the same direction once real art exists, so partial replacement will look inconsistent in the interim; that is expected and is not a reason to match the placeholder look.

## Style Pillars

| Pillar | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hand-drawn linework | Clean 2D concept-art lines with visible drawing decisions. | 3D render polish, photoreal materials, noisy edge detail. |
| Large armor shapes | Big readable chest, shoulder, bracer, hip, and shin plates. | Many overlapping micro-plates or scale-like fragments. |
| Flat/cel shading | Controlled value blocks and simple highlights. | Heavy gradients, glossy render passes, cinematic bloom. |
| One identity device | One major prop or system sells the Hero. | Several competing gadgets, capes, wings, weapons, and floating parts. |
| Phone-readable silhouette | A player can recognize the Hero and role at small size. | Detail that only works when zoomed in. |
| World-bible materials | Oathsteel, runeglass, living gold, signal cloth, aether ceramic. | Generic leather/chainmail, unmotivated glow, mundane modern gear. |

## Character Sheet Format

Preferred exploration format:

- white or light neutral background;
- one full-body 3/4 front pose;
- one optional small prop callout;
- no text, logo, UI, or fake branding;
- calm character stance unless action is specifically requested;
- clear negative space around the silhouette.

Use turnarounds only when needed. Dense multi-pose sheets are useful later, but early art direction should prioritize one strong readable design.

## Armor Shape Language

Armor should feel advanced and culture-specific without becoming visual noise.

Prefer:

- smooth ceramic-like plate groups;
- strong angular panels;
- readable shoulder/chest/forearm rhythm;
- dark bodysuit under large white armor masses;
- restrained cyan luminous seams;
- muted brass or living-gold lock accents;
- one or two signal-cloth panels.

Avoid:

- tiny trim everywhere;
- dozens of exposed hinges;
- layered skirt armor that obscures the leg silhouette;
- excessive capes or cloth strips;
- complex fantasy filigree;
- heavy battle damage unless the card specifically calls for it.

## Elian Voss Application

Elian should read as a **Shield Wall Tank** through a Gate Rig, not through a normal handheld shield.

Core visual recipe:

- sleek white and deep-navy armor;
- large simplified oathsteel plates;
- cyan runeglass seams and panels;
- living-gold lock motifs;
- one small ember-red cord from Redwater Locks;
- short gateblade baton as a key-like command weapon;
- compact Gate Rig mounted on forearm/back;
- two or three large rectangular runeglass panels when deployed.

Do not make Elian carry a giant ornate shield. The defensive identity comes from deployed gate panels and lock geometry. The rig should look operable and iconic, not like a detached decorative prop.

Recommended silhouette:

- stable, grounded stance;
- slightly broader shoulder/back mass than a damage Hero;
- compact waist and readable legs for tactical movement;
- Gate Rig asymmetry on one side;
- no excessive cape; signal cloth may hang as one or two clean panels.

## Prompt Recipe

Use this as the starting point for future Elian concept iterations:

```text
Create clean hand-drawn 2D game character concept art for Captain Elian Voss, a nonbinary Shield Wall tank Hero. Use a simplified Valorant-style character sheet feel with super-high fantasy raid technology from the world bible.

Elian wears sleek white and deep-navy oathsteel armor over a dark bodysuit, with large readable armor plates, restrained cyan luminous runeglass seams, muted living-gold lock accents, and one small ember-red cord from Redwater Locks. Their defensive tool is not a normal shield: a compact forearm/back Gate Rig deploys two or three large translucent rectangular runeglass gate panels. They carry a short gateblade baton like a ceremonial key weapon.

Style: hand-drawn line art, flat cel shading, clean anime-inspired concept art, minimal gradients, light neutral background, full-body 3/4 front pose, optional small Gate Rig prop callout.

Mood: calm, protective, deliberate, raid-role operator.

Avoid: photoreal rendering, 3D render look, layered micro-plates, excessive cape, many tiny mechanical parts, giant handheld shield, medieval chainmail, DnD tavern fantasy, generic paladin holy glow, text, logo, watermark.
```

## Art Review Checklist

Before accepting a Hero concept pass, ask:

- Does the silhouette read at phone size?
- Are there no more than one or two major visual systems competing for attention?
- Could a player name the Hero's role from posture and prop?
- Are the armor plates grouped into large readable shapes?
- Does every glow or material have a world-bible source?
- Does the art feel hand-drawn rather than 3D rendered?
- Is the design high-fantasy enough without becoming busy?
- For Elian, is the defensive identity clearly a Gate Rig rather than a normal shield?
