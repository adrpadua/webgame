## Executive Summary

**FFXIV is the only game surveyed that formally splits its healers, and the split is prevention versus restoration.** Square Enix divides healers into *pure* and *barrier*: White Mage and Astrologian restore HP directly with burst and regen and have limited mitigation; Scholar and Sage prevent damage with shields and mitigation and have less direct restoration. The split is not community shorthand — Patch 6.0 added pure/barrier as a recruitment filter in Party Finder, and Endwalker-era savage content is built for one of each. That is the single most useful fact for this project: the studio with the most explicit healer taxonomy did not choose between restoration and prevention; it mandated one of each and authored content that needs both.

**WoW has no such formal taxonomy** — only a Healer role and seven per-spec identities separated along four axes: pre-applied absorbs (Discipline), direct/burst restoration (Holy Priest, Holy Paladin), heal-over-time (Restoration Druid), and damage-to-healing conversion (Mistweaver's Eminence, Discipline's Atonement, Holy Paladin's Beacon transfer, Sage's Kardia in FFXIV). Blizzard's 2026 Midnight direction is a useful corrective to any "prevention has won" narrative: ramp healing (Discipline, Preservation) is being nerfed, Discipline's targeted/reactive tools buffed, and the stated aim is slower, less spiky, more legible healing.

**Hero shooters define support by what it does for allies, not by healing** — and as of February 2026 Overwatch is the first of them to ship an *official* sub-taxonomy: Support splits into Medic, Tactician and Survivor, each with its own passive, explicitly because one role passive could not describe a dedicated healer and a utility support equally. Marvel Rivals (Strategist) and Paladins (Support) still have no official sub-labels; "main/off healer" is community vocabulary in both.

**MOBA supports are structurally the wrong reference for a scripted co-op boss.** Riot's Enchanter and Catcher are subclasses of Controller, a PvP class defined against opponents who choose their own targets, in a lane economy that deliberately starves the support of gold, with no dedicated healer role, no threat table, and no published schedule of incoming damage. Four of their defining pressures — gold starvation, healing-must-be-counterable (Grievous Wounds), fragility-plus-grouping against target-selecting enemies, and lockdown as denial of an *agent's* choices — have no counterpart in a fight against a Boss whose next two rows are printed on the board.

## Part 1: The Five Questions

### 1. MMO healer archetypes — FFXIV formally splits them, WoW does not (confidence: medium-high for the FFXIV split, medium for per-job/per-spec mechanics)

**FFXIV's official terminology exists and is "pure healer" versus "barrier healer."** Pure healers "specialize in directly restoring HP with powerful, on-demand burst healing and healing-over-time effects" and "in return have limited access to mitigation and shielding"; barrier healers are "focused on preventing damage with more mitigation tools and on-demand shielding" and "have less access to direct HP restoration and sustained healing." White Mage and Astrologian are pure; Scholar and Sage are barrier. Three independent signals that this is first-party and not fan taxonomy: (a) Patch 6.0 made pure/barrier a selectable recruitment category in Party Finder; (b) Endwalker-era high-end duties are composed around one of each; (c) the Endwalker job design was explained in these words — Astrologian was reworked "into a pure healer" with the barrier-based Nocturnal Sect removed outright, and Sage was introduced as "a barrier healer" like Scholar, with Yoshida stating content would be balanced around a one-pure/one-barrier party.

**Answer to the taxonomy question:** the four mechanical categories the brief proposed (restorative throughput, prevention/shield, regeneration, damage-to-healing conversion) are all real and all shipped — but only FFXIV collapses them into an official two-bucket vocabulary, and it does so along exactly one axis: *does this healer stop the damage or repair it*. Conversion is not a third official bucket anywhere; it is a mechanism that both buckets use (Sage is a barrier healer with a conversion link; Discipline is a prevention spec whose conversion *is* its throughput; Mistweaver is a conversion spec with no formal label at all).

### 2. Hero Shooter support classes — Overwatch now has official sub-labels; the others do not (confidence: medium-high on the existence and names of Overwatch's 2026 sub-roles, medium on the rest)

**Overwatch.** The role is defined by empowerment rather than healing: Support heroes "empower their allies by healing them, boosting their damage, and providing vital utility." Until 2026 the only official granularity was the role itself, and "main support"/"flex support" were community terms inherited from 2016-era competitive play, not Blizzard labels. That changed: Sub-Roles were announced at Overwatch Spotlight 2026 and shipped on 10 February 2026 with Season 1: Conquest, replacing the old role passives with passives "more catered to the hero's playstyle rather than the role as a whole." Support divides into three:

- **Medic** — "dedicated healers… able to get to teammates quickly and target them with direct health recovery." Passive: self-heal for 25% of healing done to others.
- **Tactician** — Supports who "effectively use their Ultimates to turn the tide in battle, alongside providing utility." Passive: carries over excess ultimate charge after using one (up to 25%), with reduced ultimate charge gained while at full health.
- **Survivor** — Supports "built for resiliency," through offense, defense or escape. Passive: health regeneration triggered by using a movement ability.

Tank splits into Bruiser / Initiator / Stalwart; Damage into Sharpshooter / Flanker / Specialist / Recon.
Sources: [Overwatch official "Weekly Recall: Sub-Role Call!" (unfetched)](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24243646/weekly-recall-sub-role-call/), [Overwatch Wiki Sub-Roles](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Sub-Roles), [esports.gg sub-roles guide](https://esports.gg/news/overwatch/overwatch-subroles-explained/), [Turtle Beach Season 1 roles explainer](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/overwatch-season-1-all-roles-and-passives-explained), [Dexerto on main/flex being community terms](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/overwatch-2-what-does-main-flex-support-mean-2745542/).

**Marvel Rivals.** Three roles — Vanguard, Duelist, Strategist. Strategists "help and support their allies by healing, increasing damage, and using other utility skills," are "the backbone of the team," and are explicitly "not simply full healer characters": they carry buffs, utility, crowd control and respectable (if lower) damage. There are **no official sub-labels**; "main Strategist" versus "off/flex Strategist" is community vocabulary. NetEase tunes the class at role level rather than sub-role level: Season 9 (2026) raised healing and shield values across several Strategists, cut healing-to-ultimate-energy conversion from 75% to 60% and then partially reverted it to 70% when Strategists proved to be absorbing more pressure than expected, and introduced a new health type, Regenerative Shields.
Sources: [NetEase Games Marvel Rivals news](https://www.neteasegames.com/news/MarvelRivals/20250331/36929_1198455.html), [official balance post 20260711 (unfetched)](https://www.marvelrivals.com/balancepost/20260711/41667_1307328.html), [Mobalytics roles guide](https://mobalytics.gg/marvel-rivals/guides/roles-explained), [Fextralife Strategists](https://marvelrivals.wiki.fextralife.com/Strategists), [Dot Esports Season 9 patch notes](https://dotesports.com/marvel/guides/marvel-rivals-season-9-patch-notes), [Dignitas Strategist healing ranking](https://dignitas.gg/articles/ranking-every-strategist-in-marvel-rivals-by-their-healing).

**Paladins.** Four classes — Front Line, Damage, Flank, Support. Hi-Rez's own framing: "The healing and buff managing Support class provides team utility"; Supports "generally have the weakest weapons" and are "capable of healing, protecting, and boosting their allies." No official sub-labels. Differentiation is by *delivery mechanism*: Grohk heals through a placed totem (position-locked area sustain), Jenos applies a single-target heal-over-time that works through walls (line-of-sight-free sustain), Seris lands large single-target heals that splash to nearby allies, Ying heals through deployable illusions and can teleport to allies, Furia's payload is a team-wide damage/movement buff ultimate.
Sources: [Hi-Rez new & returning player guide](https://support.hirezstudios.com/en/articles/9516132-new-returning-player-guide), [Paladins Wiki: Class](https://paladins-archive.fandom.com/wiki/Class), [Paladins on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/444090/Paladins/), [Game Rant support ranking](https://gamerant.com/paladins-best-support-champions/).

**Cross-cutting:** all three define the class by *what it gives allies* (healing, buffs, utility, protection) and none defines it as "healer." Where sub-labels exist officially (Overwatch, 2026) they are cut by **playstyle and sustain-of-self**, not by healing style — Medic/Tactician/Survivor is not the same cut as FFXIV's pure/barrier, which is about whether the healing arrives before or after the damage.

### 3. Why MOBA supports differ structurally, and what does not transfer (confidence: medium-high on the taxonomy text and the economy; the transfer verdict is explicitly analysis)

Riot's taxonomy: **Controller** is the class — "defensive casters that oversee the battlefield by protecting and opening up opportunities for their allies" — with two subclasses. **Enchanters** "focus on amplifying their allies' effectiveness by directly augmenting them and defending them from incoming threats"; they are "often quite fragile," bring "relatively low damage," and "really only shine when grouped together with others." **Catchers** "specialize in locking down opponents or, in some cases, entire battlefields by creating intense zones of threat that only foolish enemies would dare wade through," and though "not as reliant on their friends as Enchanters," they "greatly benefit from allied presence." The taxonomy dates to Riot's 2016 class rework (announced April 2016, champion list June 2016, completed 2017), which renamed Supports to Controllers and added the Catcher subclass.
Sources: [LoL Wiki Controller class](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Champion_classes/Controller), [LoL Wiki Champion classes](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Champion_classes), [Fandom Champion classes](https://leagueoflegends.fandom.com/wiki/Champion_classes). These are the same definitions the repo's [champion design note](2026-08-16-lol-champion-design-lessons.md) already records.

Five structural differences from both MMO healers and hero-shooter supports:

1. **Lane economy and deliberate gold starvation.** The support does not farm; it runs a support item (World Atlas, quest-upgrading into Runic Compass) that meters gold in charges — a charge every 20 seconds, up to three, worth ~18 gold on a minion kill or on damaging an enemy champion/structure while an ally is nearby — with explicit anti-abuse throttles (heavy gold reduction past a minion threshold before 5 minutes, and past a per-minute rate after). The class is balanced around being the poorest player on the team. Nothing in an MMO or hero shooter meters a healer's power that way. Source: [LoL Wiki World Atlas](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/World_Atlas), [Dot Esports on Season 14 support items](https://dotesports.com/league-of-legends/news/lols-new-season-14-support-items-explained), [LoL Wiki Gold income](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Gold_income).
2. **No dedicated healer role.** LoL's positions are top/jungle/mid/bot/support; "support" spans enchanters, engage tanks, catchers and mages, and a team can field zero healing. Healing is one optional expression of a positional role, not a party contract. Source: [LoL Wiki Support champion category](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Category:Support_champion), [Esports Insider roles explainer](https://esportsinsider.com/league-of-legends-roles-explained).
3. **PvP target selection, and healing that must be counterable.** Enemies choose whom to focus, so the enchanter's fragility is a live tactical problem and the catcher's zone is a bet on enemy decisions. Healing therefore needs a purchasable counter: Riot's stated position (design lead PhRoXzOn) is that Grievous Wounds "should not be a necessity" and should be a situational buy against two or more heavy-healing champions — which is why Riot repeatedly cut enchanter healing and shielding to bring output in line with the counter. Source: [Riot dev post "Quick Gameplay Thoughts 1/14: Preseason 2022" (unfetched)](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/quick-gameplay-thoughts-1-14-preseason-2022/), [RiftFeed](https://riftfeed.gg/champions/riot-healing-changes), [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/league-of-legends/new-lol-healing-changes-cripple-enchanter-support-meta-1877256/).
4. **No aggro or threat table.** MMOs give one role the tools to own who gets hit; MOBAs give champions no threat value at all, so protection is positional and probabilistic rather than contractual. (Structural observation from the sources above; no first-party statement located.)
5. **No boss timeline.** Incoming damage is opponent-authored and unscheduled. That is the deepest difference: the enchanter's shield timing is a *read of an opponent*, not a *read of a schedule*, and the catcher's zone exists to change what an opponent chooses to do.

**Design lessons that do NOT transfer to a co-op fight against a scripted Boss** (analysis, stated as such):

- **Economic starvation as a balance lever.** There is no lane, no gold, no farm to deny. Healer power here is bounded by deck construction and Slot economy, which every Hero shares.
- **Healing must have a purchasable counter.** Grievous Wounds exists because a human opponent must be able to answer sustain. A scripted Boss answers only what the encounter author writes; anti-heal becomes an authored Beat, a choice — never a balance requirement.
- **Fragility-plus-grouping as the enchanter's contract.** "Only shines when grouped" is a rule about enemies picking off isolated targets. With a Boss whose targeting obeys Threat and authored Target Selectors, positioning risk is a designed variable, not an emergent PvP pressure.
- **Lockdown as the Catcher's payoff.** In PvP, locking a target denies an *agent's* choices — that is the entire value. A scripted Boss has no choices to deny. A "zone of threat only fools would enter" is an anti-player construct: against a Boss on rails, the same card resolves as either mitigation (Tank's contract) or Minion/board tempo, not as denial.
- **"Support is not a healer."** True and useful in MOBAs and hero shooters; here the Role Contract has already assigned sustain to the Healer, so importing the broader "support" umbrella would blur a boundary the encounter bible deliberately drew.

**What does transfer:** the *grammar* — every Riot subclass is written as a strength plus an explicit price — and the Enchanter's verb set (augment allies, defend allies from incoming threats), which maps cleanly onto a telegraphed damage window.

### 4. Differentiation grammar: what keeps shipped healers distinct, and what each pays (confidence: medium)

Across all five games, distinct healers are separated on five dials, and shipped healers move at least two of them:

1. **Timing** — does the output land before the damage (Discipline, Scholar, Sage) or after it (Holy Priest, White Mage, Holy Paladin)?
2. **Shape** — single-target spot (Holy Paladin, Seris), clumped-group (Restoration Shaman), spread-over-time (Restoration Druid), absorb (Discipline, Scholar), positional zone/totem (Sacred Soil, Grohk).
3. **Non-healing payload** — party damage amplification (Astrologian cards, Furia, Overwatch Tacticians), mitigation, control, personal damage.
4. **Resource machine** — lilies, Aetherflow, Addersgall, arcana, Empower charge-up, ultimate economy.
5. **The stated weakness** — every one of them has one, and in the healthiest taxonomies the studio names it.

First-party statements of intent located (all via search, none fetched):

- **FFXIV**: the pure/barrier split is not just descriptive but *enforced by matchmaking* (Patch 6.0 Party Finder categories; savage duty composition of one pure plus one barrier), with content balanced around that pairing — the strongest documented case anywhere of a studio treating healer differentiation as a party-composition contract rather than a flavor difference. Endwalker's Astrologian rework (removing the barrier sect entirely) is differentiation enforced by *subtraction*. Sources as in section 1.
- **FFXIV, forward-looking**: at Fan Festival 2026 the team presented Reborn and Evolved combat modes for 8.0/Evercold as the direct answer to jobs feeling too similar, with a stated return to intra-role specialization (the Main Tank / Off-Tank distinction named explicitly), removal of most raid-buff synergies that forced the two-minute meta, and — importantly for this note — White Mage staying a pure healer and Scholar staying a barrier healer. Sources: [MMOGah Reborn/Evolved explainer](https://www.mmogah.com/news/ffxiv/ffxiv-reborn-and-evolved-modes-explained-2026-fan-festival), [Icy Veins Evercold job changes](https://www.icy-veins.com/ffxiv/evercold-job-changes), [MMORPG.com Fan Fest conference report](https://www.mmorpg.com/news/ffxiv-director-naoki-yoshida-speaks-more-about-fan-festival-announcements-evercold-and-games-future-at-fanfest-conference-2000137901), [GamesRadar on Yoshida and over-simplification (2024)](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/final-fantasy/final-fantasy-14s-yoshi-p-says-dawntrail-will-finally-return-more-individuality-to-the-mmos-jobs-admitting-were-not-in-a-good-situation-for-that-after-years-of-over-simplification/).
- **WoW**: Blizzard removed interrupts from every healer specialization *except* Restoration Shaman, saying they "like healers having different capabilities" and that Wind Shear is an important part of the Restoration Shaman toolkit (with its cooldown raised to 30s) — differentiation deliberately expressed as asymmetric utility, plus an explicit check that solo content remains completable without it. Sources: [Blue post "Updates to Healer Specializations in Midnight" (unfetched)](https://www.bluetracker.gg/wow/topic/us-en/2189090-updates-to-healer-specializations-in-midnight/), [Icy Veins coverage](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/interrupts-removed-enemy-behavior-changed-healer-spec-changes-in-midnight/).
- **Overwatch**: sub-roles were introduced because a single role passive could not fit every playstyle in a role — passives "more catered to the hero's playstyle rather than the role as a whole." Source as in section 2.

### 5. Is a purely restorative healer still viable? (confidence: medium; evidence genuinely points both ways)

**Evidence that restorative remains a first-class design:**

- FFXIV's own taxonomy makes it structural: high-end content is composed of one pure *and* one barrier healer, so pure healing is not merely tolerated, it is required. And the pure/barrier assignment survives into 8.0/Evercold, where White Mage stays pure and Scholar stays barrier.
- WoW still ships Holy Priest as "the archetypal pure healer," Restoration Druid as the heal-over-time spec, and Holy Paladin as a reactive spot healer with best-in-class single-target output.
- The 2026 Midnight direction actively pushes *away* from ramp: Discipline and Preservation ramps were heavily nerfed while Discipline received ~25% buffs to targeted, reactive tools (Power Word: Shield, Flash Heal, Shadow Mend, Power Word: Radiance, Plea) "to improve their ability to respond to dangerous health swings," and Holy Paladin, Restoration Druid and Restoration Shaman received buffs to close the healer gap.
- Blizzard's stated reason for the pacing reform is that War Within healing "can rapidly shift raids from severely damaged to nearly full health in seconds," which is "stressful and intimidating for newer healers"; Curse of Ula'tek raised max-level player health and enemy damage by 25% so damage would be "less spiky" and "healing gameplay more satisfying." A less spiky damage profile is precisely the environment restorative healing reads well in.
Sources: [Icy Veins "Healing in Midnight is Getting Easier But at What Cost?"](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/healing-in-midnight-is-getting-easier-but-at-what-cost/), [blue post "Healing Changes in Midnight" (unfetched)](https://murlok.io/blue-posts/27842948/healing-changes-in-midnight), [PCGamesN on Midnight healer tuning](https://www.pcgamesn.com/world-of-warcraft/midnight-update-class-tuning-healer-balance), [Icy Veins Midnight beta dev notes](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/healer-burst-nerfs-big-class-tuning-ui-improvements-midnight-beta-dev-notes-december-3rd/).

**Evidence that the genre has moved toward prevention and conversion:**

- FFXIV mandates a barrier healer in the same composition rule that mandates a pure one; at the top end, prevention is not optional.
- Every current healer roster contains at least one conversion healer (Discipline, Mistweaver, Sage), and prevention/conversion specs were the ones needing nerfs in Midnight (Discipline, Preservation and Mistweaver named as the big winners that got cut).
- The criticism that lands hardest on pure healers is the empty-downtime problem. The June 2024 `#FFXIVHEALERSTRIKE` grievance list — oversimplified healer rotations, healing tools leaked to non-healer jobs, homogenization between healers, and "lack of threat level in nearly all forms of content" — describes a role whose restorative half is rarely demanded and whose damage half is one-to-two-button spam (see the [prior note](2026-08-17-healer-support-design-lessons.md) for the sourced account). The complaint is still live in 2026: the official-forum thread "For Shame, Mr. Yoshida — Being a Healer in 2026" runs to dozens of pages, with recurring themes that healers are judged on DPS rather than HPS and that tank self-sustain (Warrior especially) makes a dedicated healer redundant in casual dungeons. Source: [thread (unfetched)](https://forum.square-enix.com/ffxiv/threads/526996-For-Shame-Mr.-Yoshida-Being-a-Healer-in-2026).
- Hero shooters have converged on "the support is not a healer": Overwatch's role text leads with empowerment, Marvel Rivals' Strategists are explicitly "not simply full healer characters," and Paladins' Support is "healing and buff managing."

**Where the evidence does not settle the question.** Both directions are visible *inside the same year*: WoW is simplifying healers and reducing ramp (which favors restorative play) while drawing the same "oversimplification" criticism FFXIV received for exactly that; FFXIV is enforcing a pure/barrier pairing while its pure healers remain the ones players complain about. The honest reading is that the industry has not deprecated restorative healing — it has deprecated *empty* healing. Restorative healers stay legitimate where the encounter produces damage that could not have been pre-empted, or where the healer carries a non-healing payload worth playing. Where neither is true, restorative collapses into the green-DPS limbo the strike named.

## Synthesis: Transferable Principles

1. **The prevention/restoration axis is the one axis a studio has been willing to name.** FFXIV's only official healer taxonomy is "does the healing arrive before or after the damage." If this game names Healer Archetypes, that axis is the proven first cut.
2. **Differentiation is a composition contract, not flavor.** FFXIV made pure/barrier a matchmaking category and authored content that wants both. Differentiation that the encounter does not ask for decays into homogenization — which is grievance (3) of the strike.
3. **Every archetype ships with a named price, and the price is usually the mirror of the strength.** Prevention pays with commitment risk and bad emergencies; restoration pays by letting the damage land; regeneration pays against spikes; conversion pays in uptime and positioning; amplification pays in personal output.
4. **Conversion is a mechanism, not a class — and it dies when it stops being a decision.** Atonement (choose who carries it, when to ramp, what to convert) is the healthy form; Kardia (a flat automatic link) is the degenerate one.
5. **Sub-labels get invented when one label stops describing the members.** Overwatch shipped sub-roles in 2026 for exactly this reason. A Role with two Heroes that play differently eventually needs Archetype names — which this project already has as canonical vocabulary.
6. **PvP-derived support design carries pressures a scripted boss fight does not have.** Gold starvation, counterable healing, target-selection fragility and lockdown-as-denial are answers to a thinking opponent. Take Riot's *grammar* (strength plus price); leave its *economics and counterplay*.

## Application to This Game

**All of this section is researcher inference.** No studio has commented on this project, and the reasoning below is mine, mapped onto the repo's existing systems: the Boss Timeline's two telegraph rows (`CONTEXT.md`), Armor clearing every Round, 20-card decks, a standard Party of one Tank, one Healer and one or two Damage, the Role Contract's "Tank owns mitigation. Healer owns sustain." (`docs/rules/encounter-design-bible.md`), and the five Healer Design Principles plus the Second Hero Of A Role rule (`docs/rules/character-design-bible.md`). `Archetype` is the canonical term for a Hero's mechanical identity within a Role, with `Shield Wall` as the one named example.

### Is a Restorative Archetype legitimate here?

**Yes — but it cannot be justified the way FFXIV and WoW justify theirs, and it needs a different reason to exist.** In those games the pure healer's structural edge is answering what nobody could plan for. This game deletes almost that entire justification: the Timeline telegraphs damage a full window ahead in two rows, so there is nearly no untelegraphed damage to react to. A Restorative Archetype imported unexamined would be a healer optimized for a problem this game does not have.

Three legitimate reasons remain, and a Restorative Hero should be built on them rather than on reactivity:

- **Information advantage as the real trade.** A ward must be committed to a named window before the Beat resolves; a heal can be aimed after the board has spoken. That is a genuine, mechanically interesting asymmetry inside a fully telegraphed game: the Enchanter buys prevention at the price of guessing, the Restorative buys certainty at the price of the damage already having landed. This is the honest translation of pure-versus-barrier into a telegraphed system.
- **Attrition and residue.** Armor clears every Round and wards cover only the window they were bought for, so anything not covered accumulates as Health loss. Tank Principle 1 already states a solo tank's Health trend is monotonically downward. Repairing that accumulated floor is a job prevention structurally cannot do.
- **Recovery verbs.** Raising a `Downed` ally, cleansing authored statuses, and restoring Health after an over-budget or unmitigated hit are restorative-only jobs, and D-070's zero-health model gives the first of them real weight.

Two conditions. First, per Healer Principle 3, a Restorative Hero still needs a converting damage sub-game — and the natural converter for a restorative kit runs the *opposite* way from the Enchanter's ward: healing that overflows or exceeds need should charge something that threatens the Boss, so the restorative surplus is never wasted output. (That is our idea, not a mechanic copied from any game above.) Second, per Healer Principle 1 and the Role Contract, the encounter side must author a real residual-attrition problem; otherwise a perfectly telegraphed fight lets a well-played Enchanter reduce the Restorative to redundancy — the same no-healer-clear failure, one level down.

### If both ship, what distinct price does each pay?

Per the Second Hero Of A Role rule, the two must claim different strength-and-price patterns while both passing the Healer recognition test (both make accumulated damage sustainable):

| Archetype | Machine | Strength | Price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enchanter (ward / amplify / convert) | Pre-placed wards bound to named Incoming Beats; Boss-damage cards convert through the ward | Damage never lands; the Party's effective Health ceiling rises; best against big single telegraphed hits | **Commitment risk.** Every ward is a bet placed before resolution: a misread window, a re-targeted Beat, or an over-covered window is wasted. It cannot repair Health already lost, it cannot efficiently answer a `Downed` ally, and Armor clearing each Round means nothing it built persists. |
| Restorative (heal / AoE heal / cleanse / revive) | Direct and area healing aimed after the board resolves; recovery verbs; overflow converts into Boss pressure | Certainty and re-aimability; the only answer to accumulated attrition, statuses, and a `Downed` ally | **The damage lands first.** It cannot pre-empt a spike, so a Beat sized to drop an ally in one hit is its worst case; it has no mitigation to lend the Tank; and its throughput must sit below total telegraphed pressure so triage stays a decision (Healer Principle 5). |

The asymmetry is clean and it is the same one FFXIV enforces: one Archetype pays for prevention with waste-on-miss, the other pays for reliability with damage taken. The live risk to watch in playtest is that a fully telegraphed Timeline makes the Enchanter's bet too easy to win, which would leave the Restorative as the strictly worse pick. Two levers exist without breaking the telegraph: Beats whose Target Selector is only resolved at resolution time, and the bounded untelegraphed pressure the encounter bible already permits (at most one Echo per phase transition, plus Escalation and Hazard/DoT residue).

### Does the Catcher belong to the Healer Role at all?

**My reading: no — and the prior note's healer roadmap should be corrected on this point.** The Catcher is defined by locking down opponents and creating zones of threat "that only foolish enemies would dare wade through." Both halves of that definition presuppose an opponent who makes choices: the value is denying an agent's decisions. A scripted Boss on a published Timeline has no decisions to deny. Against it, a Catcher card resolves as one of three things, none of which is sustain:

- reduced or relocated damage — which is **mitigation**, and the Role Contract gives mitigation to the Tank;
- Minion and board tempo — a Damage or Tank axis, and one the encounter bible already handles via simultaneous-occupation and add-density problems;
- an answer to a specific authored mechanic — which `CONTEXT.md` already models as a **Responsibility**, explicitly orthogonal to Role and never tied to an Archetype.

So the Enchanter/Catcher pair, imported wholesale from a PvP game, is the wrong sibling pair for this Role. The Healer's siblings should be cut on the axis the one game with an official healer taxonomy actually uses — prevention versus restoration — giving **Enchanter versus Restorative**, exactly parallel to the Tank's Warden (`Shield Wall`) versus Vanguard split.

One nuance worth preserving: a Healer may still own *spatial* delivery. Scholar's Sacred Soil is a healer-owned zone, and a zone that heals or mitigates allies standing in it is sustain delivered spatially — inside the contract. What is outside the contract is a zone whose only output is denying movement or locking Minions. If a control-flavored Healer is wanted later, the honest framing is "an Enchanter whose wards are placed on hexes rather than on allies," not a Catcher.
