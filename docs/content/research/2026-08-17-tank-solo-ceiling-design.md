# Research: Why Tanks Cannot Solo — the Design of the Solo Ceiling

Date: 2026-08-17
Method: Deep-research harness (26 agents) — the search phase identified the right primary sources but every fetch was egress-blocked, so zero claims passed adversarial verification. Findings below were assembled from convergent search-index snippets across independent outlets (medium confidence), plus one source of **first-party, reproducible evidence: this repo's own headless evaluation sweep** (`npm run evaluate`), which independently produced both canonical lone-tank failure modes against the real engine.
Scope: Why tanks cannot solo group content in MMOs (WoW, FFXIV), why single spirits struggle in Spirit Island, why Sentinels of the Multiverse wants 3+ heroes — and the transferable principles for tank heroes in this game under D-016 (solo Boss kills are tuning defects).

## Executive Summary

Across MMOs and co-op board games, the lone tank is designed to fail in exactly two ways, and healthy encounters keep both walls live simultaneously: the **stalemate** (survive forever, never win — closed by enrage timers and DPS checks) and the **race** (win the damage exchange but die first — closed by attrition exceeding self-sustain). The tank-healer split is an economy: the tank converts spike damage into a survivable *stream*; only the healer owns the budget that pays for the stream. Where games hand tanks a full sustain budget (FFXIV's Warrior), the healer role visibly erodes — the same failure the healer research documented from the other side. And the deepest lesson is Rextroy's: **numeric ceilings erode under optimization** (stat-stacking through +500% Berserk), while **structural ceilings hold** — no gear level solves a tower that needs four bodies, a stack marker that "will typically kill whoever is targeted if they are alone," or a Spirit Island board a single spirit's presence cannot reach. Our own evaluation sweep is the local proof: range camping let a bot pick its failure mode freely (97% solo kills or a 100% untouchable stalemate) because nothing in the encounter yet forces the lone tank to hold *both* axes at once.

## Findings

### 1. The tank-healer contract is an economy, not flavor (medium confidence)

The tank's job is converting incoming damage into a survivable stream — positioning, mitigation windows, cooldown rotation against tank busters ("one of Rampart or a 40% cooldown, plus a short cooldown"). The healer owns the sustain *budget* that pays for that stream. Tank self-healing exists but is deliberately sized as an emergency valve, not a budget: "healers are more or less solely responsible for keeping the party alive." The counterexample proves the rule: FFXIV's Warrior, "the tank with the best sustain... easily completes dungeons even without any support from the healer" — widely cited in role-balance complaints, and the same erosion pattern as WoW's delve healer-tank companion being nerfed as "too effective." Sources: [Icy Veins tanking intro](https://www.icy-veins.com/ffxiv/introduction-to-tanking), [TheGamer role guide](https://www.thegamer.com/final-fantasy-xiv-healer-dps-tank-role-beginners-guide/), [Sportskeeda on the delve nerf](https://www.sportskeeda.com/mmo/wow-delves-too-easy-healer-tank-brann-combo-blizzard-explains-nerf).

### 2. Enrage exists to close the stalemate; attrition closes the race (medium confidence)

Community and design discussion states it directly: "hard enrages are DPS checkpoints, and without them you could have poor DPS and still beat encounters if your healers are above average" — i.e., without a clock, survival stacking wins. "Raids are a test of all 3 components, not just tanks and healers; thus enrage timers exist," and the structure prevents "bringing 100 healers and taking your time." The two designed lone-tank failure modes follow: out-survive but time out (stalemate), or out-damage but die first (race). Sources: [MMO-Champion enrage thread](https://www.mmo-champion.com/threads/1105620-Enrage-timers), [SE forum enrage-design thread](https://forum.square-enix.com/ffxiv/threads/401326).

### 3. Numeric ceilings erode; structural ceilings hold (medium confidence)

Rextroy's catalog of solo raid-boss kills — Maut through a 500% Berserk with 160% stacked Versatility, a current-tier boss in green gear via a specific trinket over a two-hour fight — demonstrates that any *numeric* solo ceiling eventually falls to dedicated optimization ("using broken mechanics they intentionally put into the game"). Meanwhile FFXIV's Echo "only affects player stats and does not impact enemy health or encounter mechanics" — old content becomes soloable precisely where the barrier was numbers, yet mechanics that require bodies stay hard: stack AoEs "split damage among all participants and will typically kill whoever is targeted if they are alone," towers where "missing towers often causes huge raid damage or instant wipe," spreads punishing overlap, mechanics targeting whole role groups. No stat total occupies two hexes. Sources: [Wowhead on the Maut solo](https://www.wowhead.com/news=317628/rextroy-solos-maut-through-500-damage-increase-berserk-with-160-versatility-from), [Icy Veins on the Rik Reverb solo](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/rextroy-solos-current-tier-raid-boss-rik-reverb-in-green-gear-after-2-hour-fight/), [Gamer Escape common mechanics](https://ffxiv.gamerescape.com/wiki/Common_Mechanics), [FFXIV wiki on the Echo](https://ffxiv.consolegameswiki.com/wiki/The_Echo).

### 4. Threat scales with time; actions scale with actors (medium confidence)

The board game evidence is the cleanest statement of the action-economy law. Sentinels scales villain output by H (hero count) yet three heroes still wins less often than four or five — scaled-down damage does not compensate for lost card throughput. Spirit Island scales *linearly* per spirit (one board, fear and blight thresholds per player), and true-solo is "swingy and not as friendly to every spirit, especially narrow ones": one spirit's presence and range cannot cover even one board's simultaneous escalation, and complementary fast/slow, offense/defense pairings are what multi-spirit play buys. Pandemic escalates infection draws over time against a fixed four actions per turn. The pattern: **threat per turn is a function of the encounter and grows; actions per turn are a function of actor count and do not.** A lone actor falls behind the escalation curve by construction — that is what escalation is *for*. Sources: [BGG on Sentinels' H](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/898354/clarification-of-h-used-villain-powers), [Steam discussion on hero counts](https://steamcommunity.com/app/337150/discussions/0/615086038673601338/), [BGG on Spirit Island scaling](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2043801/difficulty-scaling-inversely-number-players), [Beyond Solitaire solo review](https://www.beyondsolitaire.net/blog/solo-review-spirit-island), [Games Precipice on player-count scalability](https://www.gamesprecipice.com/player-count-scalability/).

### 5. First-party evidence: our own sweep produced both failure modes (high confidence — reproducible)

`npm run evaluate` (30 seeds × 12 policies, real browser engine): `sword_shield/far` survives the full clock untouched in 100% of seeds (the stalemate, uncloseable because nothing punishes range camping); `dual_steady/far` solo-kills Embermaw in 97% (the race, winnable because `boss_damage` has no range check and attrition can be opted out of). The lone tank was never forced to hold survival and damage *simultaneously* — the exact structural gap findings 2-4 predict. Backlog issue 15 (counter-pressure against range camping) is the named fix.

## Proposed Tank Design Principles (for the character design bible)

Researcher inference, grounded in the findings and this game's numbers:

1. **Sustain is a stream, not a budget.** The tank's kit converts hits into survivable form (Armor sized to the hit, wiped each round) but must own no meaningful Health income. Attrition per round — unavoidable Tank Hits — must exceed what mitigation can fully blank *on average*, so perfect play slows the bleed and never stops it. The Warden makes the hit survivable; only a Healer makes it sustainable. Guard this against sustain creep (the Warrior lesson): D-008 already forbids Armor conversion; extend the instinct — healing in tank kits stays trivial (`Rallying Cry` at 2) or becomes party-directed.
2. **Both walls stay live.** The enrage clock closes the stalemate; authored attrition closes the race. Solo tuning target: optimal solo play loses to *both* — cannot out-survive 8 rounds (attrition floor) and cannot out-damage the Boss's health within them (solo damage ceiling). Today neither wall holds against range camping; that is the P0.
3. **Make the ceiling structural, not numeric** (the Rextroy rule). Numbers erode under optimization; bodies-in-places hold. The levers already in our vocabulary: role-load-bearing Beats (healer-only and damage-only counter tags), and hex "towers" — telegraphed hexes that must be *occupied* at resolution or the party pays — which translate FFXIV's most solo-proof mechanic directly onto our board. A lone Elian can be unkillable and still unable to stand in two hexes.
4. **Escalate threat past one actor's economy.** One hero has two Slots and one window income; the encounter's simultaneous demands (front to hold, cone to dodge, Whelps accumulating with visible intent, a tower hex to fill) must grow past that. Inert Whelps are why the escalation curve is currently flat — Whelp intent (D-006) is solo-ceiling work, not polish.
5. **The lone-tank loss must read "I need my team," not "I am weak."** The tank should visibly *win their axis* — prevented damage credited on the HUD and in records — while the board fills with demands labeled for absent roles: Whelps piling up that a Damage hero would clear, chip Health loss a Healer would cover, an unmet tower. The Round-4 checkpoint framing already does this; presentation should attribute the eventual loss to the empty party slots, never to the tank's performance.
6. **Party scaling raises the walls, not the tank.** Boss health and demand density scale with party size (per the Ashen Trial frame); the tank's own numbers stay constant across party sizes so mastery transfers and the solo slice remains an honest diagnostic.

## Caveats

- Zero claims survived adversarial verification — every fetch was egress-blocked, so all external findings are snippet-sourced (medium confidence). The harness *did* locate the right primaries for a later archive pass: The Balance's tanking guide, the Warcraft wiki enrage-timer page, Icy Veins' tanking introduction, daniel.games/scaling, and Raph Koster's trust-spectrum essay.
- Community statements about *why* designers chose these structures are inference from behavior and patch notes, not designer quotes, except where noted.
- Our sweep's bots are simple policies; human play would shift magnitudes but not the structural conclusion (both failure modes reachable, neither wall enforced).

## Suggested Next Steps

1. Fold the six principles into `docs/rules/character-design-bible.md` (pending review) as a Tank Design Principles section beside the Healer one, and cross-reference D-016.
2. Elevate range-camping counter-pressure (backlog issue 15) to the top of pre-playtest engine work — it is the enforcement mechanism for principles 1-2, and the sweep gives it a regression metric (`victory%⚠` and `enrage%` for `far` policies must both fall).
3. Design the hex-tower Beat pattern (principle 3) as the first structural multi-body mechanic — it needs the Party model eventually, but its solo form ("unoccupiable demand") already creates honest pressure.
4. Give Whelps end-of-round intent (D-006) so escalation outpaces one actor's economy (principle 4).
