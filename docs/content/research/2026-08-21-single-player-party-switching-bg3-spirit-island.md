# Research: One Player Controlling Multiple Party Members — BG3 and Spirit Island: Digital Edition

Date: 2026-08-21
Question: How do Baldur's Gate 3 (Larian) and Spirit Island: Digital Edition (Handelabra) let a single player control and switch between multiple party members/spirits in single-player — the switching gesture, the "who am I controlling" signal, the interaction with turn structure, the fate of pending mid-turn actions, and the swapping of per-character hand/resource panels?
Method: Search-index research only. **Direct fetches were refused by this session's egress proxy** — `bg3.wiki`, `baldursgate3.wiki.fextralife.com`, `store.steampowered.com`, and `handelabra.com` all returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED` (the same posture the [healer taxonomy note](2026-08-20-healer-support-taxonomies-mmo-hero-shooter.md) documented for a different domain set). So: ~12 targeted searches, claims accepted only where multiple independent results converged, and per-claim sources given as the URLs the search index surfaced. **No source page below was opened directly**; quoted text is what the search tool returned as quotation and may be paraphrase of the underlying page. Larian publishes no controls manual at a stable public URL that the index surfaced, so BG3 UI claims rest on the two community wikis (bg3.wiki, Fextralife — both document the actual shipped UI) and controls-reference sites; Spirit Island claims rest on Handelabra's own site/FAQ/beta pages, the Greater Than Games rulebook as mirrored by rules references, and launch-window reviews.

Confidence is labeled per section and capped at **medium-high** throughout because nothing was verified against a primary page. "Application to This Game" is researcher inference, not any studio's statement.

## Executive Summary

**BG3 and Spirit Island solve two different problems.** BG3's problem is *who is the camera-and-hotbar avatar right now* inside a sequential initiative order; its answer is instant free switching (portrait click / F1–F4) with the turn system deciding when switching is legal, and a deliberately compressed initiative die (a d4, not D&D's d20) that makes allied characters land adjacent in the order often — which the engine then merges into one freely-interleavable group turn of up to 10 creatures. Spirit Island's problem is *how to route one player through work that the tabletop rules say happens simultaneously*; its answer is to serialize the simultaneous — one consolidated Spirit Phase panel shown for one spirit at a time, then a shared pool of played power cards that the player resolves in any order they choose across all their spirits.

**The pending-action story is the sharpest contrast.** In BG3, switching away from a character mid-turn (within a grouped turn) costs nothing: unspent actions, bonus actions and movement persist and the player can switch back until that character's turn is explicitly ended. In Spirit Island digital, there is nothing to lose by "switching" because commitments are phase-scoped, not character-scoped: cards are committed (and paid for) in the Spirit Phase, then *resolved* later card-by-card in any order, with per-spirit undo inside the Spirit Phase and skippable powers at resolution time.

**For a game where several characters act inside shared Quick/Slow windows, Spirit Island is the load-bearing precedent** — commit-then-resolve, whole-panel swap per character, order-free resolution inside the window — while BG3 contributes the interaction-cost lessons: one-keystroke switching, camera snap-to-selected (and the player complaints about it), and the value of never punishing a switch.

## Part 1: Baldur's Gate 3

### 1.1 The switching gesture (confidence: medium-high)

- **Portrait click.** Party portraits are stacked on the left edge of the screen; a single click on a portrait switches control to that character. Multiple guides state this identically. Sources: [Magic Game World](https://www.magicgameworld.com/baldurs-gate-3-how-to-switch-characters/), [PCGamesN camera controls](https://www.pcgamesn.com/baldurs-gate-3/camera-controls), [Gamepur](https://www.gamepur.com/guides/how-to-switch-party-members-in-baldurs-gate-3).
- **Hotkeys F1–F4** select party members 1–4 directly (not a cycle through a single key); rebindable in the options menu. Sources: [keyboardcontrols.com BG3 reference](https://keyboardcontrols.com/shortcuts/games/baldurs-gate-3/), [Gamerant hotkeys](https://gamerant.com/baldurs-gate-3-best-hotkeys-most-important-bg3/).
- **Double-click portrait (or double-tap F1–F4)** additionally makes the camera *follow* that character. Sources: [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/baldurs-gate-3/camera-controls), [Steam community discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/0/3274690571073738580).
- **Controller:** L2 opens party management — cycling through party members, switching the controlled character, and grouping characters together; the radial-wheel UI replaces the mouse hotbar entirely. Source: [Gamepur controller guide](https://www.gamepur.com/guides/baldurs-gate-3-controller-guide-button-layout-shortcuts-more).
- **Switching works in unusual contexts:** during combat when it isn't a character's turn (to inspect positions), and even during dialogue (other members can act while one talks) — the dialogue case regressed at least once post-launch, per a Larian forum bug thread. Sources: [Gamepur](https://www.gamepur.com/guides/how-to-switch-party-members-in-baldurs-gate-3), [Gameranx on mid-conversation switching](https://gameranx.com/features/id/474473/article/baldurs-gate-3-how-to-change-characters-during-conversations/), [Larian forums bug report](https://forums.larian.com/ubbthreads.php?ubb=showflat&Number=954963).
- **A second, orthogonal mechanic — grouping/linking — governs who follows whom while exploring.** Portraits show chain links; drag a portrait away until the chain "breaks" to detach a character, right-click a portrait for Group/Ungroup, dedicated group/ungroup buttons sit under the portrait stack, and `G` re-links everyone to follow the leader. Ungrouped characters stand still when not selected. Sources: [EIP Gaming split-party guide](https://eip.gg/bg3/guides/split-the-party/), [DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/baldurs-gate-3-how-to-split-your-party/), [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/how-link-unlink-party-members-baldur-s-gate-3), [Prima Games select-all](https://primagames.com/gaming/baldurs-gate-3-select-all-party-members). This is inherited near-verbatim from Divinity: Original Sin 2's chain/unchain portrait mechanic (see Part 3).

### 1.2 Signaling who is controlled (confidence: medium)

- The selected character's portrait is the visually distinguished one in the left-edge stack (guides consistently direct players to the portraits as the source of truth for selection; the wikis document the portrait UI). *Inference from converging guide language; no single source spells out the exact highlight treatment.*
- **Camera behavior is the loudest signal:** selecting a character snaps/centers the camera on them, every time, with a settings option controlling how snap-back is handled on leaving combat. Players complain that the mandatory recenter forces re-panning — evidence that camera-jump-on-switch is a real cost when switching is frequent. Sources: [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/baldurs-gate-3/camera-controls), [Dot Esports camera guide](https://dotesports.com/baldurs-gate/news/baldurs-gate-3-camera-controls-guide-all-camera-controls-in-bg3-explained), [Steam community complaint thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/0/3010053344561426597).
- **The whole bottom HUD swaps** to the selected character (see 1.5), so panel identity is itself a selection signal.

### 1.3 Turn structure: compressed initiative plus grouped consecutive turns (confidence: medium-high)

- **Initiative is a d4 + Dexterity modifier** (not tabletop 5e's d20), rolled per participant at combat start. Source: [Fextralife Initiative page](https://baldursgate3.wiki.fextralife.com/Initiative). The compressed die keeps rolls in a narrow band, so allied characters (who often have similar modifiers) frequently land adjacent in the order. *The adjacency-frequency consequence is inference; the d4 fact is stated.*
- **Grouped turns:** "Allied creatures usually take their turns at the same time when they are in close proximity to each other and are directly adjacent to each other in the turn order, with up to a maximum of 10 creatures able to take their turns interchangeably"; uncontrolled summons are excluded. Source: [bg3.wiki Turn-based mode](https://bg3.wiki/wiki/Turn-based_mode) (via search snippet).
- **Within a grouped turn the player switches freely and interleaves actions:** move character A, cast with B, strike with A again, reposition B — any interleaving before ending turns. Turn-order portraits along the top of the screen show the grouping. Sources: [Gamer Guides initiative explainer](https://www.gamerguides.com/baldurs-gate-3/guide/gameplay/getting-started/surprise-rounds-and-initiative-explained), [GamesRadar combat guide](https://www.gamesradar.com/baldurs-gate-3-combat/), [Steam community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/0/3811785047382773613).
- This grouped-simultaneous design is Larian's fix for Divinity: Original Sin 2's strictly-alternating initiative, and commentary frames it exactly that way. Source: [DualShockers analysis](https://www.dualshockers.com/baldurs-gate-3-combat-fixes-divinity-original-sin-biggest-problem/). In co-op the same mechanic lets multiple *humans* act simultaneously — the single-player switching surface and the multiplayer simultaneity are the same system. Source: [Larian forums thread on disabling simultaneous turns](https://forums.larian.com/ubbthreads.php?ubb=showflat&Number=878472).

### 1.4 Pending actions on switch-away (confidence: medium-high for persistence; medium for End Turn scope)

- **Switching away mid-turn is free.** Within a grouped turn, each character's unspent action/bonus action/movement persists across switches; the player can return and spend them at any point until that character's turn is ended. Sources: [chaptercheats PS5 hint (describes the interleave-and-return loop)](https://www.chaptercheats.com/cheat/playstation5/600167/baldurs-gate-3/hint/178708), [Steam community discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/0/2969524751703035566).
- **End Turn is the destructive commit, and its scope is a documented pain point.** One Steam thread states that hitting End Turn before selecting the second grouped character "assumes that you do not want that character to do anything and skips" onward ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/0/2969524751703035566)); a Larian forum thread asks for End Turn to pass control to un-acted grouped allies instead of ending the shared turn ([Larian forums: Skipping shared turns](https://forums.larian.com/ubbthreads.php?ubb=showflat&Number=747065)). **Flagged as user-reported and possibly patch-dependent** — the search index did not surface an authoritative statement of End Turn's exact scope in grouped turns, and behavior may have changed across patches. A cancel window exists ("PSA: You can cancel an end turn", [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/0/3819669231699015578/)).
- Design takeaway (inference): BG3 makes *switching* costless and concentrates all commitment into one explicit, per-character (but scope-ambiguous) End Turn action — and the one place players report losing pending resources is exactly where that commit's scope was unclear.

### 1.5 Panel swap on switch (confidence: medium-high)

- The bottom-of-screen hotbar is per-character: each character's actions, spells, items, spell-slot and resource readouts are populated from *their* spellbook and inventory, customized per character (drag from spellbook, per-hotbar lock toggle). Switching characters swaps the whole assembly. Sources: [ProGameTalk hotbar guide](https://progametalk.com/baldurs-gate-3/hotbar-guide/), [Gamerant hotkeys](https://gamerant.com/baldurs-gate-3-best-hotkeys-most-important-bg3/) (documents hotbar-focused keybinds), community hotbar mods existing precisely because per-character hotbar management at scale is painful ([Better Hotbar 2](https://www.nexusmods.com/baldursgate3/mods/2417)).
- On controller, the equivalent is per-character radial wheels cycled with L1/R1. Source: [Gamepur controller guide](https://www.gamepur.com/guides/baldurs-gate-3-controller-guide-button-layout-shortcuts-more).
- Inventory/character-sheet screens open in the context of the selected character (and the party inventory shows four columns, one per member). *Converging guide descriptions; treated as uncontroversial.*

## Part 2: Spirit Island: Digital Edition

### 2.1 What the tabletop rules require the digital edition to implement (confidence: medium-high)

The Greater Than Games rulebook structures every round as five phases: **Spirit Phase → Fast Power Phase → Invader Phase → Slow Power Phase → Time Passes.** In the Spirit Phase each spirit, in this order, Grows (choose one growth option from the spirit panel), Gains Energy (highest uncovered number on the energy presence track), and Plays and Pays for Power Cards (up to their card-plays track limit). All players act **simultaneously within each phase**; power speed is printed on each card (red ring = Fast, blue = Slow); "Powers may be resolved in whatever order the players want, so long as no Power interrupts another partway through," and a power may be skipped at resolution (energy not refunded; elements still count). Sources: [UltraBoardGames rules mirror](https://www.ultraboardgames.com/spirit-island/game-rules.php), [Spirit Island Wiki: Sequence of Play](https://spiritislandwiki.com/index.php?title=Sequence_of_Play), [Spirit Island Wiki: Powers](https://spiritislandwiki.com/index.php?title=Powers), [RulesPal rulebook](https://www.rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook), [official-rules mirror](https://officialgamerules.org/game-rules/spirit-island/).

So the digital edition's design problem is stated by the rules themselves: the tabletop assumes N brains working in parallel and negotiating order verbally; the digital edition must sequence one brain (or N remote brains) through nominally simultaneous work.

### 2.2 The switching gesture and phase sequencing (confidence: medium, assembled from Handelabra pages and launch reviews)

- **Solo multi-spirit play is a first-class advertised mode:** "play a solo game with 1 to 4 Spirits," plus pass-and-play and (since the 2022 multiplayer update) cross-platform online play where "each player must control at least one Spirit, and may control more than one Spirit." Sources: [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1236720/Spirit_Island/) (via search snippet), [Handelabra multiplayer beta page](https://www.handelabra.com/spirit-island-beta), [Handelabra Spirit Island page](https://www.handelabra.com/spiritisland).
- **Spirit Phase: serialized, one spirit's consolidated panel at a time.** A launch review describes the core compromise: in tabletop "every player executes the growth stage of the spirit phase simultaneously," but "there's simply too much information to display all at once, so Handelabra had to create a consolidated version of it… only one person can actively view their hand or player board at any given moment." Source: [WayTooManyGames review](https://waytoomany.games/2020/11/29/review-spirit-island-digital-edition/). The full spirit panel (growth options, presence tracks, innate powers) is a per-spirit screen; growth is chosen "at the upper-right of the Spirit Panel" per spirit. Sources: [GeekDad review](https://geekdad.com/2020/08/geekdad-review-spirit-island-digital/), [Spirit Island Wiki: Spirit](https://spiritislandwiki.com/index.php?title=Spirit).
- **Fast/Slow Power Phases: order-free resolution across all controlled spirits.** The digital edition implements the rulebook's "any order" rule — the player resolves played cards and unlocked innate powers one at a time in whatever order they choose across every spirit they control, and may skip a power at resolution. Rulebook basis: [Spirit Island Wiki: Powers](https://spiritislandwiki.com/index.php?title=Powers), [UltraBoardGames](https://www.ultraboardgames.com/spirit-island/game-rules.php). Digital confirmation is indirect (reviews describe resolving powers card-by-card; the multiplayer page describes exactly this loop for N players — see next bullet). *The precise on-screen affordance (tap a played card to begin resolving it) is inference from converging review descriptions, not a fetched primary statement.*
- **The multiplayer version of the same problem shows the concurrency model explicitly:** "During the Fast and Slow Power Phases, all players can use Powers simultaneously, but while you are using a Power, nobody else can use another Power that could affect the same Lands or Spirits" — i.e. land/spirit-scoped locking — plus group votes for decisions the table must agree on. Source: [Handelabra multiplayer beta page](https://www.handelabra.com/spirit-island-beta). In single-player multi-spirit, the one player simply *is* every lock-holder in sequence.
- The gesture for inspecting a spirit's full panel outside the flow: right-click / double-tap (documented at least for the New Game screen). Source: [Handelabra Helpshift FAQ on Power Progression](https://handelabra.helpshift.com/hc/en/3-spirit-island/faq/229-how-can-i-turn-off-power-progression-to-choose-my-own-power-cards/).

### 2.3 Signaling which spirit is active (confidence: medium, partly inference)

- Because the Spirit Phase is serialized, "which spirit am I acting for" is communicated structurally — the whole visible hand/panel belongs to exactly one spirit at a time (the WayTooManyGames observation above). Card details render small and enlarge on highlight to keep a full hand on screen. Source: [The Friendly Boardgamer review](https://thefriendlyboardgamer.wordpress.com/2020/05/24/spirit-island-digital-final-thoughts/).
- During power resolution, the acting spirit is identified by the power being resolved (each card belongs to a spirit); the board itself is shared and never swaps. *Inference from the game's structure; no source articulates it.*

### 2.4 Pending/incomplete actions (confidence: medium-high — this is where Handelabra's own documentation is strongest)

- **Commitments are phase-scoped.** Cards are chosen and paid for in the Spirit Phase; nothing "resolves" until the matching power phase, so moving attention between spirits inside a phase abandons nothing.
- **Fine-grained, per-spirit undo:** the multiplayer update introduced "fine-grained Spirit Phase undo, allowing Growth and other actions in the Spirit Phase to be undone using the buttons directly on their interfaces," with each spirit's Spirit Phase actions **independently undoable** when one player controls multiple spirits, and a main undo that rolls back to the start of the Spirit Phase or to the last point new information was revealed. Source: [Handelabra multiplayer beta page](https://www.handelabra.com/spirit-island-beta).
- **At resolution, a power may be skipped** rather than half-executed (energy spent stays spent; elements still count) — the rulebook rule the digital edition enforces. Sources: [Spirit Island Wiki: Powers](https://spiritislandwiki.com/index.php?title=Powers), [Querki Spirit Island FAQ](https://querki.net/raw/darker/spirit-island-faq/Spirit+Phase).
- Net effect (inference): the game never holds a dangling per-character action in the BG3 sense; the undo boundary is "new hidden information revealed," not "you looked at a different character."

### 2.5 Hand/resource panel swap (confidence: medium-high)

Each spirit owns a complete, disjoint state bundle that the UI presents per spirit: hand of power cards + cards played this turn + discard, energy pool, the two presence tracks (energy/turn and card-plays/turn, where uncovering slots raises income and play limits), element tally from cards played, and unlocked innate power thresholds. The digital edition shows this as the consolidated per-spirit panel/hand described in 2.2, swapped whole when the player moves to another spirit; the island board is the only shared surface. Sources: [GeekDad review](https://geekdad.com/2020/08/geekdad-review-spirit-island-digital/) (presence tracks uncovering energy/card-plays), [UltraBoardGames](https://www.ultraboardgames.com/spirit-island/game-rules.php) (energy gain = highest uncovered track number), [WayTooManyGames](https://waytoomany.games/2020/11/29/review-spirit-island-digital-edition/) (one hand/board visible at a time), [The Friendly Boardgamer](https://thefriendlyboardgamer.wordpress.com/2020/05/24/spirit-island-digital-final-thoughts/) (interface clarity, card zoom).

## Part 3: Side-by-side

| Axis | BG3 | Spirit Island: Digital |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Switching gesture | Left-edge portrait click; F1–F4 direct-select; double-click/tap to camera-follow; controller L2 party wheel | No free-roaming "selection" — Spirit Phase serializes spirits one panel at a time; power phases let the player pick any played card of any spirit to resolve next |
| Control signal | Selected portrait + camera snap-to-character + whole HUD swap | Whole visible hand/panel *is* one spirit's; board is shared and never swaps |
| Turn structure | Sequential d4 initiative; allies adjacent in order merge into one freely-interleavable grouped turn (≤10 creatures) | Five fixed phases; all spirits act "simultaneously" per phase; digital serializes Spirit Phase and makes Fast/Slow resolution order-free |
| Pending actions on switch | Persist fully within grouped turn; destroyed only by explicit End Turn (whose group scope is user-reported as a footgun) | Nothing dangles: commit (pay/play) in Spirit Phase, resolve later in any order; per-spirit undo until new info revealed; powers skippable at resolution |
| Panel swap | Per-character hotbar, spell slots, resources, inventory context | Per-spirit hand, energy, presence tracks, elements, innates; only the island is shared |

## Part 4: Other one-player-pilots-a-party precedents (one line each)

- **Divinity: Original Sin 2 (Larian, 2017):** origin of the portrait chain/unchain follow mechanic BG3 inherited — drag portraits apart to "unchain" a character for independent control ([Steam community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/435150/discussions/2/3223871682615607791/), [TheGamer party commands](https://www.thegamer.com/divinity-original-sin-2-party-commands/)) — but with strictly alternating initiative, no grouped turns ([DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/baldurs-gate-3-combat-fixes-divinity-original-sin-biggest-problem/)).
- **Gloomhaven digital (Flaming Fowl, 2021):** a *commit-then-sequence* pattern distinct from both mains — solo player secretly picks two cards per mercenary up front, then the revealed initiative numbers dictate the acting order, so "switching" is scheduled by the cards, not chosen at will ([EIP Gaming review](https://eip.gg/reviews/gloomhaven-review-iconic-board-game-re-united-with-its-inspiration/), [Age of Miniatures review](https://ageofminiatures.com/gloomhaven-pc-review/)).
- **Darkest Dungeon (Red Hook, 2016):** the null case — one player owns all four heroes but never "switches": a per-round speed roll (d8 + SPD) picks whose turn it is and the player simply issues that hero's one action ([Darkest Dungeon wiki: Combat Mechanics](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Combat_Mechanics_(Darkest_Dungeon))).

## Application to This Game

Researcher inference from here on. Our situation — one player piloting a Tank and a Healer through shared Quick/Slow action windows on a hex grid — is structurally Spirit Island's (simultaneous phase windows, per-character resource bundles), not BG3's (sequential initiative that merely *sometimes* produces shared turns). But BG3 is the better-tested interaction layer. What transfers:

1. **Commit-then-resolve beats select-then-act for shared windows.** Spirit Island's phases work solo because intent (cards played, energy paid) is separated from resolution (any order, skippable). If our Quick window has both characters declare/queue actions and then resolve in player-chosen order, switching mid-window is automatically safe — there is no dangling half-action to lose. BG3 shows the alternative's cost: free-form interleaving works, but demands an explicit per-character End Turn whose group scope confused players and ate un-acted turns.
2. **Take BG3's gesture, not its structure.** Portrait click plus one direct-select hotkey per character (F1/F2-style, not a cycle) is the converged standard; with only two characters, a single toggle key is even better. Double-activate-to-follow is a proven overload of the same surface.
3. **Whole-panel swap, shared board.** Both games swap the entire per-character surface (hotbar/spell slots vs hand/energy/presence) and keep exactly one shared surface (world/island). With two characters we can afford what neither could: showing *both* resource summaries at once and expanding only the active one — directly addressing the "only one hand visible at a time" complaint Spirit Island's consolidated Spirit Phase drew.
4. **Signal control by panel identity and a portrait state, and be gentle with the camera.** BG3's mandatory camera recenter on every switch is its most-complained-about switching behavior; on our single-boss hex arena, both characters are usually in frame, so switching should move the camera little or not at all — a highlight ring on the hex and portrait is enough.
5. **Make the window, not the character, the commit boundary.** Spirit Island's per-spirit undo bounded by "new information revealed" is the right undo model for a deterministic-until-the-boss-acts game: freely undo either character's queued Quick actions until the window resolves against the boss's revealed behavior, then lock.
6. **Sequencing inside the window is a feature, not overhead.** Spirit Island's order-free resolution is where multi-spirit solo play gets its combo depth (Fast setup into Slow payoff across spirits). Our Tank-taunts-then-Healer-shields orderings inside a Quick window are the same design material; the UI should make resolution order an explicit, visible choice, as the played-cards row does.

One caution: Gloomhaven's pattern (pre-commit both characters' full turns, then watch initiative sequence them) is the strongest tension-generator of the surveyed designs but removes exactly the mid-window adaptability that makes piloting two roles feel like piloting; it fits a harder "programmed" mode more than the core loop.
