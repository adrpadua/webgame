# Design Proposal: Deepening the Riposte Payoff

Date: 2026-08-17
Status: Option A adopted (user-approved, D-015) and implemented. Option B proposed as its successor for the Party-scale pass. Option C held. Option D rejected.
Context: [Champion design research note](../research/2026-08-16-lol-champion-design-lessons.md) (impact/counterplay proportionality, mastery values), the controlled deck evaluation (`docs/artifacts/deck-eval-notes/2026-08-13-elian-voss-controlled.md`), and the MMO build/spend loop survey summarized below.

## Problem

Riposte Ready is Elian's earned payoff state, but as originally shipped it was a 1-bit proc with a single spender: only Shield Slam (2 copies in 20 cards) could consume it. The controlled cohort showed the consequence — the payoff converted in one of three seeds. Three distinct weaknesses:

1. **Whiff:** earning the proc with no Shield Slam in hand or Slot meant the reward silently evaporated — the worst feeling in proc design.
2. **No depth of earning:** every perfect block paid identically; repeated mastery did not compound.
3. **Flat spend:** `+2` with no timing decision, no overcap tension, no hold-versus-cash choice.

## The MMO build/spend vocabulary

| Pattern | Canonical examples | Decision it creates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Builder/spender points | WoW combo points, Holy Power; FFXIV job gauges | Spend early for tempo vs. ride to cap; overcap wastes |
| Role-income resource | WoW Warrior Rage (generated by being hit), Revenge procs | Doing your job pays your offense |
| Proc window | Revenge/Overpower after a block or dodge | React inside a short window or lose it |
| Refund-on-prediction | FFXIV Dark Knight's The Blackest Night | Correct prediction, not just execution, is what pays |
| Decay/momentum | GW2 adrenaline; Kessa Varn's proposed Momentum | Keep acting or bleed value |

The tank-canonical loop is role-income: get hit correctly → gain resource → spend on an empowered ability. Riposte Ready is that loop with a 1-bit resource.

## Options

### Option A — widen the spenders (ADOPTED, D-015)

Any card that deals Boss damage consumes Riposte Ready when it resolves: Shield Slam gains the full `+2`; any other Boss-damage card gains `+1`. Cards without Boss damage (Iron Guard, Fortify, Sweeping Blow's Minion damage) never consume it, so defensive play inside the window stays free.

- Kills the whiff: an earned window always converts to something.
- Adds spend-order mastery: firing Steady Strike first cashes the window cheaply; holding for the Slam is the stronger, riskier line. This is a real decision, not a trap — the design accepts that a rushed `+1` is the price of impatience.
- Costs zero new engine nouns; the settled non-stacking, non-refreshing, expiry rules are unchanged.
- Implemented in both engines with a distinct lifecycle reason per consumption kind (`matching_card_fired` vs `boss_damage_card_fired`) so Encounter Records distinguish full payoffs from cheap cash-outs.

### Option B — promote Riposte to a counted resource (PROPOSED successor)

"Resolve," cap 3: each qualifying zero-loss Tank Hit builds one; Shield Slam spends all stacks for `+2` per stack; other Boss-damage cards spend one stack for `+1`. A true builder/spender: riding to cap across Rounds becomes a plan, the enrage clock pressures early spends, and repeated perfect blocks compound.

- **Amends settled rules** (non-stacking was deliberate, to keep Shield Slam from becoming automatic) and must *replace* the binary status, not sit beside it, to keep the engine-noun count flat per the complexity budget.
- Gate: decision-log entry, engine seams (stack tracking, per-stack spend), focused probes, and deck-evaluation evidence of no dominant always-Slam line before promotion. Target window: the Party-scale pass.

### Option C — refund-shaped payoff (HELD)

A perfect block returns one charged card from the Guard stack to hand: the payoff becomes card economy, feeding the deck's four-currency routing machine directly. On-brand for a Warden, but less legible than a damage number and inflationary if untuned. Held as a candidate signature for a future third tank-adjacent Hero rather than a change to Elian.

### Option D — Armor-to-resource conversion (REJECTED)

"Leftover Armor converts to a resource" is the obvious Rage import, but it violates D-008 (Armor is a pure damage shield, never spent for another effect) and would corrupt the machine's central lesson that overstacked Armor is waste — Armor-as-a-precise-bid is Elian's mastery curve.

## Implementation Record (Option A)

- Web engine: `web/src/engine/types.ts` (`bonusBossDamageOffPayoff`), `statuses.ts` (riposte carries `+1` off-payoff), `resolve.ts` (consumption keys on the fired card dealing Boss damage; graded bonus; distinct lifecycle reasons). Tests added for off-payoff `+1` consumption and non-consumption by no-Boss-damage cards; suite green (55 tests).
- Godot engine: `scripts/sdk/StatusEffect.gd` (`bonus_boss_damage_off_payoff`, gated `outcome_for`), `scripts/sdk/EncounterEngine.gd` (graded consumption with lifecycle reasons), `scripts/Main.gd` (status line, tooltip, and payoff feedback name the graded rule and the consuming card).
- Probe: `scripts/debug/riposte_ready_probe.gd` gains `_test_off_payoff_consumption` (Steady Strike cashes `+1` with the recorded reason; Iron Guard does not consume). **Never executed, and it will not be** — the Godot build is frozen (ADR 0019) and no longer carries verification for this rule. The two Godot bullets above are a record of where Option A was implemented in the frozen client, not outstanding work; the web engine's own tests are what keep this rule honest.
- Card and status text: `shield_slam` rules text already names the full payoff; the general rule lives in the Riposte status line and tooltip per the authoring rule that shared semantics belong in the rules reference and inspection experience, not on every card.
- Docs: Elian design doc (core loop, settled rules, content rules, evidence), starter deck doc, prototype rules, character design bible Elian table, Embermaw tutorial player-language, decision log D-015.

## Evaluation Consequences

- The rules change invalidates prior evaluation cohorts as evidence about the *current* rule: the 2026-08-13 baseline and controlled cohorts remain historical evidence for the old rule only. `controlled_deck_eval_probe.gd` re-runs the fixed seeds and asserts a legal Shield Slam payoff occurs; under the new rule a replayed seed may cash its riposte early on a Steady Strike, changing the trace. If the probe fails on regeneration, that is the expected new-cohort boundary, not a defect: regenerate the controlled records under the new rule and re-review per the rubric.
- The next evaluation should specifically watch the new failure mode Option A introduces: if seeds show the `+1` cash-out is *always* taken immediately (never held for the Slam), the hold-versus-spend decision is not real and Option B's economy — or a smaller off-payoff bonus — should be revisited.
