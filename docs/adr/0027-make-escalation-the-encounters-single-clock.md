# Make Escalation the encounter's single clock

Every Boss carries `Escalation`, a counted value on one fixed scale from `0` to `5`. It gains `1` at the end of each Round once automatic escalation begins, and gains more from authored per-Beat penalties when a demand goes unanswered. Each value from `1` to `4` carries an authored `Escalation Threshold` effect that changes how the fight behaves; the threshold at `5` is the hard wipe. `End-of-Clock Behavior` is restated as that top threshold's effect rather than a parallel rule, and the term `Enrage Timer` retires into `Encounter Clock`'s list of words to avoid. `Encounter Clock` now means the number of Rounds that automatic ticks alone need to reach the top threshold — still eight before modifiers, but now variable in one direction, because a party that leaves demands unanswered arrives sooner.

**Automatic escalation starts late, by derivation.** A fixed `0`–`5` scale ticking from Round 1 would be a five-Round clock, which contradicts the eight-Round Encounter Clock every piece of the solo slice is calibrated against. The reconciliation is that automatic ticks begin at `roundLimit - 4`, so ticks alone reach `5` exactly when the clock expires: on Embermaw they land at the ends of Rounds 4 through 8, thresholds `1`–`4` take effect during Rounds 5 through 8, and the wipe fires at the end of Round 8 — the same moment the old round-limit check ended the fight. Three properties made this the right reading rather than shortening the clock or making the scale per-Boss (rejected below). It is boundary-identical to the previous behavior, so this is an additive change and D-017's tuning evidence survives. It leaves Rounds 1 through 4 unescalated, so the Round-4 checkpoint cannot regress for structural reasons. And it puts the collapse in the late fight where the research's phase model wants it, instead of spending the thresholds during the teaching Rounds. Acceleration is deliberately not gated the same way: from Round 1, leaving a demand unanswered adds Escalation and therefore pulls the collapse forward, which is the one lever that makes throughput a responsibility rather than a preference.

The problem is that a binary Round limit gives the party a clock they can read but never feel, and gives the Damage role nothing structural to own. The cooperative-boss research is direct about this: a soft enrage should precede the hard boundary so the approaching collapse is experienced rather than merely counted, and graduated pressure is what converts throughput from "bigger numbers" into ownership of how much of the encounter's worst state ever occurs.

The stronger reason to adopt it is what it does to the solo ceiling. D-016 requires that a solo Hero cannot defeat a Boss, and D-017 currently holds the stalemate wall with a health value — 48 on Embermaw — chosen so eight Rounds are not enough. A health number is exactly the kind of ceiling the tank research shows gets eroded: better play, a sharper deck, or one more efficient policy chips at it until the wall moves. Graduated escalation makes indefinite defense fail because the encounter's economic assumptions become impossible, which no amount of optimization repairs. That is the same structure-over-tuning test the Encounter Design Bible already applies to cooperation, applied to the clock, and it is why Escalation lands in the solo slice now rather than waiting for the Party model: the solo slice is the only place we currently have automated adversarial pressure to prove it.

Three alternatives were rejected. An automatic tick alone is a second clock with extra steps, and leaves Damage owning nothing. Acceleration alone — Escalation gained only from unanswered demands — lets a flawless party stall forever, reopening the exact stalemate loophole this decision exists to close, so the automatic tick stays as the termination guarantee while acceleration is where the Damage role's contribution lives. A per-Boss threshold count was rejected because Escalation is a literacy surface: feeling a clock requires reading it without arithmetic, and a per-Boss scale forces the party to re-learn what `3` means on every encounter. Boss identity therefore lives in the effects at each threshold, where there is unlimited room, not in the length of the bar.

Acceleration is authored on each Beat, with no global rule. A global rule — any live Minion at Round end adds `1`, any uncleared Hazard adds `1` — would make every add package equally punishing and take away an author's ability to make one scarier than another, flattening Boss identity into a single attrition formula. What keeps per-Beat authoring legible is mandatory disclosure, which closes cleanly with two decisions already made: a Beat that can add Escalation must say so in its Forecast disclosure, and because an Escalation Threshold crossing is one of the three run-ending outcomes in D-025's test, such a Beat is `Severe` by definition and ADR 0026's ladder already sends it to the Forecast Row. No special case is needed to make those three rules agree.

Two rules fell out of building it, both narrowing acceleration rather than the tick. **A Minion that arrived this Round is not an unanswered demand.** Whelps spawn in the Incoming Row, so no player window can reach them before the Round-end step; counting them would make acceleration a second automatic tick rather than a consequence of a choice, so only a Minion that survived a full Round counts. And **Embermaw authors its Brood Call penalty at `0` for now**, because D-003 forbids promising `Kill Adds` while the live deck holds no executable Minion answer. Pricing a demand the deck cannot answer is the same defect from the other direction. The mechanism ships complete and is proven against a catalog variant that does price it; the authored value rises to `1` when the Whelp-clearing card lands. The first sweep made the case concrete: with the penalty live, every policy escalated about one extra step per Round and the eight-Round fight collapsed to five or six Rounds for reasons no player could have prevented.

The accepted cost is that this is the only law from this pass that changes live behavior. Adopting Escalation on Embermaw regenerated the evaluation sweep and the committed Scenarios, and prior cohorts are historical evidence for the old rule — the pattern already established at D-015 and D-019.

Acceptance has two standing criteria and one dated observation, and the difference matters — see the correction at the end of this ADR for what happens when they get muddled.

The **standing criteria**, which must hold on every sweep: `victory%` stays at `0` across every policy, so D-016's wall is intact; `checkpoint%` stays identical to the pre-change baseline on all twelve pre-Escalation policies (`100/100/100/100/0/0/100/100/100/100/13/43`), which is the structural protection the late tick start was chosen for; and **at least one policy reaches the Encounter Clock and dies to Escalation having dealt `0.00` Boss damage.** That last one is the enrage wall stated in evidence rather than asserted — a line that survives everything the Boss does still cannot kill it, and now something ends the fight anyway. It is what the survival-biased `turtle` policy (Iron Guard in Quick, Fortify in Slow, no offense) was added to produce.

The **dated observation** is which policies carry it and how often, and that is a snapshot of current tuning, not a property of the design.

**Amended 2026-08-17 (D-031): thresholds may be structural.** Values `1` through `4` may now permanently change the arena instead of raising a number, and Embermaw's two damage-bonus thresholds became permanent Scorch — `1` Ashen Verge burns the western edge, `4` Closing Jaws spreads around both western corners, so the ground closes toward the Boss as the fight ages. The counted top threshold is unchanged and stays the termination guarantee: a purely structural end condition can stall if the board never degrades, and the automatic tick is the only reason the fight is guaranteed to end at all.

The amendment exists because revision 2 of the cooperative-boss research caught a defect rather than proposing an addition. Thresholds `1` and `4` were both "attacks deal `+1`" — the numeric inflation the Encounter Design Bible forbids, authored two commits after that rule was written. Structural effects also need a different implementation from the read-time modifiers: permanent terrain change is a mutation, so it rides generated `apply_hazard` actions when the threshold is crossed, and a Hazard may now be marked permanent so the Round boundary does not clear it.

One authoring ban travels with the mechanism: **no authored Scorch hex may be adjacent to the Boss.** Scorching the Guarded Front would leave the Tank unable to move into their own answer while Raking Claw's unguarded bonus applied every Round — the same defect as pricing an unanswerable demand, arriving from the other direction. A test asserts it over live content rather than trusting the author.

The sweep held all three standing criteria. One claim is explicitly not yet evidenced: burning the western edge should punish Boss-range camping (D-017), but far-camping policies die before the burn lands, so the effect is unmeasured rather than proven.

**Corrected 2026-08-17: the recorded percentages had gone stale, and the record was the wrong shape.** This ADR originally stated acceptance as "`turtle` reaches Round 8 in 67–80% of seeds and dies to Escalation in 43–67%", then D-031 revised it upward to 67–90% and 53–80%. Both readings were true when written and neither survived contact with other people's work: PR #42 raised Embermaw's Phase II damage (Raking Claw `4`→`6`, Cinder Breath `5`→`7`), so survival-biased policies now die to attrition before reaching the clock more often, and the figures fell to 0–67%.

Nothing about the design changed. What changed is a number nobody had promised to hold, recorded as though acceptance depended on it — so a routine damage tune silently falsified an ADR. The fix is not fresher digits; it is separating the criteria that must hold on every sweep from the observation that happens to be true this week. A gate that decays whenever someone tunes damage is not a gate.

So the third criterion is now **counted by `npm run evaluate` rather than described here**: the sweep tallies runs that reached the Clock and died to Escalation at `0` Boss damage, and reports a red flag if that count is zero, alongside D-016's solo-victory flag. It is asserted for the full sweep only — a `--policy` subset cannot be held to a sweep-wide property, since most single policies legitimately never reach the clock. Verified in both directions: the full sweep counts `49` qualifying runs, and running `turtle/stay` alone (which dies to attrition around Round 6) produces zero and trips the flag.

Measured at `ce9ff21`, 30 seeds × 18 policies: `victory%` `0` everywhere with no red flags; `checkpoint%` unchanged on all twelve prior policies; `turtle/dodge` and `turtle/dodge/spike` reach Round 8 on 67% and 73% of seeds and die to Escalation on 67% and 60%, both at `0.00` Boss damage. The standing criterion is met with room to spare, by two policies rather than one.

Two details in that snapshot are worth reading, because they are about the design rather than the tuning. `turtle/stay` never reaches the clock at all — holding the Guarded Front against an unavoidable claw is fatal long before Round 8, which is Tank Principle 2's race wall doing its job on a policy that brings no throughput. And `turtle/far` reaches the clock more often than it dies to Escalation, meaning it is still dying to attrition out at range; the anti-camping burn from D-031 remains unmeasured for the same reason it was unmeasured then.

**Amended 2026-08-17 (D-036): acceleration is live, and the deck could always answer it.** Embermaw's Brood Call now carries `escalation_if_unanswered: 1`. This ADR shipped the mechanism authored at `0` and deferred it to "when the Whelp-clearing card lands" — but that card was already in the deck. The starter list holds two copies of Sweeping Blow: `2` damage, range `1`, piece-targeting, against a Whelp with `2` Health. Driven through the live engine it kills one per fire, three attempts for three kills. D-003's gate was satisfied the whole time and nobody had re-checked the premise, so the one lever that makes the clock respond to play sat switched off through the entire pass that argued for it. Across 540 sweep runs before this amendment, `escFromAdds` was `0.00` on all eighteen policies: Escalation had never once accelerated in measured play.

The measured consequence is the relationship the mechanism was for. A survival-biased policy that ignores adds now dies to the clock around Round 6–7, while `culler` — the added policy that spends its Quick Slot on Sweeping Blow and walks to the Whelps — is the only policy in the sweep that reaches Round 8 at all. Paying the adds tax is what buys the full clock, which is throughput as a responsibility rather than a preference, stated in evidence.

The blanket-zero test that guarded this went with it, because it was the same class of defect as the stale percentages above: `expect(escalation_if_unanswered).toBe(0)` reads like D-003 but only encodes one encounter's tuning, and it would have kept passing forever while the premise rotted. It is replaced by D-003's actual rule — *if any Brood Call is priced, the encounter deck must contain a card that can kill the Minion it spawns* — so pricing and answerability cannot drift apart again.

One figure is worth recording as an observation, not a criterion: the deepest solo push now leaves Embermaw at `4` Health of `48`, where it previously left about `10`. Differentiating the programs (D-036) lowered per-Round damage, so Heroes live longer and deal more. `victory%` is `0` across 1,200 runs at 400 seeds, so D-016 holds — and it holds for the reason this ADR wanted, since every one of those runs ends at Escalation rather than at Boss Health. The health number is no longer the binding wall, which is the outcome the decision was arguing for. If a solo victory ever appears, the fix is the clock, not the health total.

**Amended 2026-08-17 (D-039): the Scorch ban is about authored hexes, not player-caused ones.** The ban above reads "no authored Scorch hex may be adjacent to the Boss," and the word *authored* is now load-bearing rather than incidental. A threshold's `scorch_hexes` are fixed coordinates the party cannot influence, so burning the Guarded Front with one would price a demand nobody can answer — that is what the ban exists to stop, and the test over live content still enforces it.

Ash Trail burns the Guarded Front too, and it is the opposite case. The hex is chosen by where the Tank chose to stand, and whether it lands under them or behind them is decided by whether they absorbed the hit. Twice-earned is not unanswerable. The Guarded Front is also a ring rather than a hex, and `turn_toward_player` re-aims it at the Tank every Round, so a burnt front hex rotates the Tank around their own answer instead of locking them out of it.

The ruling, so the ban is not quietly reinterpreted later: **authored Scorch may never sit adjacent to the Boss; player-caused Scorch there is a consequence and is the point.**
