# Give each Hero a fixed Signature Slot with earned Charges

Every Hero gains a third Action Bar Slot that is always present. Its Top Card is printed on the Hero — never in the deck, never drawn, never replaceable at Loadout, never discarded — and carries two clauses. The **standing clause** watches for one authored event and, when its gates pass, grants the Slot one Charge. The **activation** fires in its printed window like any Slot: at least one Charge required, once per window, and on firing it spends its whole Charge Stack while its Top Card stays. Charges are **earned only** — a hand card can never be tucked beneath a Signature — and bank across Rounds up to the card's printed `max_charge`, with a trigger while full earning nothing. The design decision is D-057 (minted as D-050, renumbered with the decision-log collision fix); the full settled shape, the rejected alternatives, and the first increment (migrating Elian's Riposte) live in [fixed-hero-power.md](../content/design-proposals/fixed-hero-power.md). This ADR records the two architectural constraints the Slot creates and the one it must not break. The migration shipped 2026-08-19 with its evaluation cohort; since ADR 0033 the Slot renders in the Hero Frame rather than on the bar.

## The first exception to ADR 0008

ADR 0008's contract is that activation never consumes a Charge Stack, and that a Slot firing at its full Charge Value discards its Top Card and stack at the window's end. The Signature inverts both halves: **its Top Card never discards, and firing always spends the whole stack.** This is deliberate, not drift. ADR 0008's cleanup exists so that a deck-sourced module cycles — investment, payoff, replacement — and the Slot Tension it prices is the pressure of choosing what to install. A Signature is not installed and cannot be replaced, so cleanup has nothing to cycle; and because its Charges arrive from play rather than from hand, spending the stack is the payoff cadence rather than the loss of an investment. ADR 0008 anticipated "future class-specific full-charge mechanics"; this is that hook exercised, as an exception scoped to `fixed: true` cards only. Deck-sourced Slots keep ADR 0008's rules unchanged.

## Earned charge is what protects ADR 0002

ADR 0002's Slot Tension — too few Slots for the cards worth installing, and every tuck a real spend — is the main pressure on the player's hand economy. A fixed Slot that accepted hand charges would drain that economy into itself and decay the other two Slots into battery feeders. The constraint is therefore structural, not tuned: **the legality layer refuses `prepare`, `replace`, and `charge_slot` against a fixed Slot.** Hand cards physically cannot flow into the Signature, so the normal Slots keep their fuel and their tension, and the Signature's rate is bound to the authored encounter — the Boss decides how often the standing clause can possibly fire. That structural bound is also the D-016 posture: the solo ceiling stays governed by authored cadence rather than by a number that better play erodes.

ADR 0002 reserved a third Slot for progression. The Signature first shipped as a third bar position, moving that reservation to a fourth Slot; since ADR 0033 (D-058) the Signature renders in the Hero Frame instead, the bar carries two Slots again, and the progression reservation is back on the third.

## Everything authorable in `data/`

A Signature is a card — `fixed: true`, plus a `standing` array — not a new component kind, so it inherits `speed`, `max_charge`, `boss_damage`, `target_type`, and Charge Modifiers unchanged (ADR 0020). The standing clause is a **Grant**: the mirror of a Counter Reader, answering one event from the same closed `when` set, narrowed by `event_keyword` exactly as D-049 narrows Readers, behind a closed gate list where every gate must pass and no boolean combination exists — the same line D-047 drew between a reader vocabulary and a query language. A Grant produces one Charge where a Reader produces a number; nothing else is new. Catalog validation admits `fixed: true` only on a card a Hero references.

This retires the last hero mechanic living in engine code. D-033 kept Riposte Ready in TypeScript because its graded consumption authored badly; per-Hero Signature plus earned charge leaves one payoff route and no Counter, so nothing graded remains and `createRiposteReady()`, the `SHIELD_SLAM` card-id constant, the grant branch in `resolve.ts`, and the four engine-only `CounterInstance` fields all delete with the migration. D-015 is superseded with its diagnosis intact: banked Charge cures the evaporating proc at the root where the graded cash-out only priced it.

## Accepted costs

The Signature adds a permanent object to a 390×844 portrait surface that ADR 0031 just fought to keep sparse; it shipped first as a narrow third bar plate, and since ADR 0033 (D-058) it renders as the Hero Frame's Signature control instead — off the bar entirely, which is the sharper answer to "must read as different from the two replaceable Slots". Its earn moment must be Board Feedback rather than a silent state change. Earned Charges need a token representation — `charges: CardInstance[]` assumes a tucked card, and that assumption is the one genuinely new engine structure. And printing the payoff on the Hero changes what a deck is for: setup, conversion, and fuel, with Armor sizing literally the earn rate for Elian — the Character Design Bible's revision carries that reframing.

One number is flagged rather than accepted: banking to `max_charge: 2` creates a `7`-damage single-window ceiling where today's is `5`. The banking-vs-cashing prototype showed the cash-at-one line dominating the bank line under the migrated numbers alone; the ruling recorded in the proposal (its decision 13) answered with a full-bank rider — fired at the cap, the activation also places `Sundered` — which required one addition to the authored vocabulary (a `full_charge` effect block on `fixed` cards) and none to the constraints above.
