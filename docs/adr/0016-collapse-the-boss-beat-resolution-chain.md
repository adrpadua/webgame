# Collapse the Boss Beat resolution chain

`EncounterResolver`, `EncounterSnapshot`, and `EncounterResolution` are gone. `TimelineResolver.resolve_boss_beat` owns each Boss Beat kind's spatial rule and its conversion into generated actions in one place, reading engine state directly. It remains an internal collaborator below the engine seam (ADR 0014); Boss Beats still enter the rules as first-class `RESOLVE_BOSS` actions through `apply`.

The chain was four files and six hops around 38 lines of rule: `actions_for_track` built a `RESOLVE_BOSS` action, resolution called back into `TimelineResolver`, which copied eight engine fields into an `EncounterSnapshot`, handed it to `EncounterResolver`, read seven fields back off an `EncounterResolution`, and re-expressed them as actions. The two DTOs — 24 fields of interface around less implementation than that — existed only to keep `EncounterResolver` engine-free, a purity nothing depended on once ADR 0009 made the engine itself the headless, scene-free authority. One output (`log_suffix`) had no consumer at all and is deleted with the chain.

This closes out ADR 0007: the value it identified — Boss Timeline spatial rules verified without a scene — is preserved, but the seam that delivers it is the engine's own (`apply` on an authored Beat), not an internal resolver with hand-built snapshots. The `boss_beats` Probe asserts every authored Beat contract (Raking Claw's targeted Tank Hit on and off the former arc, Ash Trail, Cinder Breath's hit, miss, and full-pattern scorch, Turn's facing snap, Brood Call's spawn selection) through that seam, and that no chain module exists beside the `TimelineResolver`.
