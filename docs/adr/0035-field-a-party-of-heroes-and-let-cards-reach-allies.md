# Field a Party of Heroes, and let cards reach allies

An Encounter fields a **Party**: an ordered list of seats, each naming a Hero from `data/heroes/` (ADR 0034), a start hex, an optional per-seat decklist, and whether that Hero's Signature is installed. `party` replaces the Encounter's single `hero`, `player_start`, and `fields_signature`; a solo fight is a Party of one rather than a different kind of Encounter, which is the point of making the seat a list. CONTEXT.md's Party is two to four Heroes and the schema allows one to four, because the teaching slice and the Ashen Trial are solo and stay that way.

Three rules follow from there being more than one Hero, and each is the answer to a question that had no meaning before.

**A Boss Beat aims at a Role.** `target_selector` has been an authored, validated `role` Keyword since D-044, and until now it was only ever *recorded* on the fact — with one Hero there was nothing to select. It now picks the Hero the Beat lands on, and every spatial rule the Beat runs — its facing turn, its Guarded Front check, its unguarded bonus, its displacement — reads that Hero's coordinates rather than the primary seat's. Ties break by authored seat order, because it is the only tiebreaker stable across seeds and a Boss whose target depends on the RNG is one no party can plan against; `Threat` (CONTEXT.md, not yet in the engine) is the real answer and replaces this rule rather than extending it. A selector matching no living Hero falls back to the first seat and records `target_selector_fell_back` on the fact, because an off-composition party must still be hittable, and a Beat quietly landing on the wrong Role is the kind of thing that reads as a balance mystery three cohorts later.

**A card can reach an ally.** `ally` joins `none`, `hex`, `board_slot`, and `piece` as a target family: a living party member within the card's range, who receives that card's Armor, healing, and Counter instead of the firing Hero. It is its own family rather than a widening of `piece` because `piece` means *an Enemy* everywhere else in the rules — legality, range, Counter hosting — and overloading it would make every existing target check ask which kind of piece it meant. The firing Hero is a legal `ally` target: covering yourself is a worse use of the card, never an illegal one, and refusing it would leave a solo Party unable to fire its own preservation cards. This is the seam the Healer role is blocked on, and nothing else in the game was going to ask for it.

**A seat carries its own deck.** A Hero's Role is not stored anywhere — it is read back out of the Role Keyword every card in their deck carries (ADR 0034). Two seats sharing one decklist are therefore two seats with the same Role, and a Beat selecting by Role could never tell them apart: a party of one Hero played twice. A seat's `deck` is optional and falls back to the Encounter's `player_deck`, which is what every solo Encounter authored before the Party existed and what evaluation decks still override.

## What this deliberately does not do

**Downed and Revive are not implemented.** CONTEXT.md defines both fully — a Hero at zero is a blocking, non-targetable body, revivable by an adjacent ally discarding a card, with the Encounter ending only when every Hero is Downed at once — and the engine still ends the Encounter the moment any Hero reaches zero. Implementing the canon here would have changed the fight's difficulty in the same commit that changed its shape, and there is no party content to measure the change against yet. The selector already skips a Hero at zero so that rule stays correct when Downed lands.

**No UI.** The Workbench still renders and drives the primary Hero only. `primaryHeroId` survives beside `partyHeroIds` precisely because presentation asks "whose frame is this?" far more often than it asks for the roster, and ADR 0033 already shaped the Hero Frame as the party-frame seed. A second frame, hero switching, and ally target selection are the next increment, deliberately held so the design system pass can settle what they look like first.

**No Threat, no committed intents, no Encounter Responsibilities.** Each is separately gated in the backlog and none is needed to put a second Hero on the board.

**No authored party content.** These are seams. The two authored Encounters are still solo, and every one of the 438 existing tests passes unchanged — which is exactly why the party rules needed their own suite: the existing tests would pass whether or not any of this were wired up.
