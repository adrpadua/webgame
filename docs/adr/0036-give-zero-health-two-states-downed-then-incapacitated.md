# Give zero health two states: Downed, then Incapacitated

A Hero reduced to `0` health becomes **Downed** — a blocking, non-targetable body on their hex, keeping the Counters they hold. Any living Hero adjacent to them may **Revive** them by discarding one hand card, returning them at an authored fraction of maximum health. If nobody does so by the end of the following Round, the body leaves the board, its Counters leave with it, the Hero's hand is discarded, their deck is never consulted again, the Party takes one Escalation charge (`unanswered_rescue`), and the Hero becomes **Incapacitated**: still at the table, choosing one of three ally-facing actions each Round, never removed from play. The Encounter is lost when every seated Hero is Downed or Incapacitated at once. Downed requires another living Hero to exist: a Hero reduced to zero alone is defeat immediately.

## Why two states rather than one

The single state this replaces was "Downed, then permanently defeated and removed from play", and it fails the fifth Healer Design Principle — **no blame-sink** — at both ends. A Hero waiting inert to learn whether an ally judged them worth saving is the purest blame-sink co-op design has; a Hero then removed from play converts one bad Round into a person with nothing to do for the rest of the fight. A preservation Role cannot be built on top of that: every triage decision the Healer makes would be a decision about whether to eliminate a teammate.

`Sentinels of the Multiverse` is the closest analogue and answers this directly. A Hero at `0` HP is *incapacitated* — cards removed, character card flipped — and thereafter uses one of three abilities printed on the flip side each turn. Incapacitated Heroes cannot regain HP, because they no longer have HP: there is no revive at all. The design keeps the **player acting** rather than keeping the **Hero alive**, and that is the half worth importing. What is not worth importing is the absence of a rescue: our Boss Timeline telegraphs damage a full window ahead, which makes a bounded rescue window the highest-stakes proactive decision the Healer owns. So the window is kept and expiry leads into the acting state rather than into removal.

`Duelyst` was considered and is the wrong reference, which is itself informative: its General at `0` Health simply ends the game, because the General *is* the loss condition rather than a party member — closer to a king in chess than to a Hero. Its transferable idea is `Dying Wish`, a death trigger on minions, which belongs to Minion design and not here.

## What this supersedes

**ADR 0035's deferral.** That ADR states the engine ends the Encounter the moment any Hero reaches zero, and names the deferral deliberate: implementing the canon would have changed the fight's difficulty in the same commit that changed its shape, with no party content to measure against. That reasoning held and this ADR is its resolution, not a correction of it. ADR 0035's forward-looking guess also holds: `selectBeatTarget` already filters to Heroes above zero health, so the selector needs no change.

**CONTEXT.md's "permanently defeated".** The term is retired. Nobody is removed from play, so a term implying removal would mislead every future reader.

## The constraints that keep it honest

- **A body answers nothing.** A Downed Hero satisfies neither a Beat's Role selector nor the proximity demand. Without the second half, a party could park a corpse beside the Boss and dodge the D-041 Escalation charge for the rest of the fight — being far *is* the failure that demand exists to price, and a body is not a Hero standing in reach.
- **The charge is for the failed rescue, not for the fall.** `unanswered_rescue` bills at the window's expiry, mirroring `unanswered_minions`, which bills a Minion for surviving a full Round rather than for spawning. A Hero going Downed is a demand; failing it is the failure. Billing at the fall would double-punish one blow.
- **Counters need no new rule.** D-045 already says a Counter never outlives its host and leaves when the host leaves the board. The body is on the board while Downed and gone at Incapacitation, so a rescued Hero keeps their ward and an abandoned one loses everything riding on them — derived, not invented.
- **The deck stops.** Draws are seeded. A deck that kept drawing for a Hero who cannot play would move every later draw in that seeded line and silently invalidate comparison against earlier Encounter Records; stopping it keeps a Party's fingerprint stable across the transition.
- **The diminished actions never touch the Boss's health.** Three actions — `grant_ally_armor`, `ally_draws_card`, `reduce_escalation` — all aimed at allies. An Incapacitated Hero who could still push the kill is a Hero the Party has little reason to rescue, which would quietly invert the rescue demand into an option.
- **They are named for the mechanic, not the flavour.** `bossBeatSchema` already states this rule for Beat kinds — *"Beat kinds name the mechanic, never one Boss's flavour for it; `title` and `rules_text` carry the flavour"* — and it holds identically here, for the same reason: a flavoured identifier welds one Hero's voice into a shared contract, and the moment a second Hero wants a different word for the same effect the name is either wrong or duplicated. The first draft shipped `steel_the_party`; it is a fine thing to *call* `reduce_escalation` on screen and the wrong thing for the rules to call it. This is the boundary between a **working name** — the id, which every debug surface, fact stream, and test reads — and a **flavour name**, which rides a title. Content ids for one specific thing (`iron_guard`, `elian_riposte`) are allowed their flavour precisely because they name one thing; a member of a closed set that many Heroes share is not.
- **`reduce_escalation` is the only effect in the game that moves the clock backwards**, which makes it the diminished set's one balance-relevant unknown. It is deliberately small and worth measuring before the set grows.
- **The revive fraction is content.** A bare percentage compiled into the engine is the mistake `range_tiles` was before D-043: the number that decides how forgiving the whole game is, living where no designer can reach it.
- **Solo is not a degenerate case.** Downed requires a living ally. This keeps both authored solo Encounters behaving exactly as they do today, so the change lands without retuning the Ashen Trial or invalidating a single existing Encounter Record.

## Accepted costs

Three actions shared by every Hero is the weaker half of the Sentinels pattern, whose whole appeal is that each Hero's incapacitated abilities say something about that Hero. Per-Hero authored abilities are the growth path and are deliberately not built: exactly one Hero is authored today, so there is nothing to differentiate against, and D-064 is the precedent for shipping the container with one consumer first.

A reduced Party can still win, which sits against D-016's rule that any solo victory is a **red flag**. The rule is kept — the comeback should not be forbidden — but the evidence pipeline must treat a reduced-Party victory as the same signal a solo victory is, because it is the same evidence: the missing Hero was decorative. That is the no-healer-clear test wearing a different hat, and the sweep has to grow the check.

This is comparable in size to the Party seams rather than a follow-up to them: it touches resolution, the proximity demand, a new Escalation reason, a new action kind, hand and deck lifecycle, board entity removal, and the glossary.
