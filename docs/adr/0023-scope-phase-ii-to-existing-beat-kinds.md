# Scope Phase II to existing Beat kinds

Embermaw gains the Phase Trigger authored in `docs/content/encounters/embermaw-ashen-trial-design.md` — a Phase Break at 18 Health or the start of Round 5 — but Phase II is built from the six Beat kinds that already exist, at harder values plus the unguarded-Guarded-Front bonus (ADR 0021). The approved Conflagration package is explicitly deferred: Ashen Brand and Cinderstorm need delayed markers and board-origin patterns, and Molten Tail needs rear arcs, displacement, and a collision policy, which are ranks 4 and 5 of the Engineering backlog and are out of the current scope.

The problem being solved is that three Boss Programs loop unchanged for the whole encounter, so Round 7 plays like Round 2 and the fight never escalates. A Phase Reveal that swapped to an existing Program with no change in pressure was rejected as a flourish a playtester reads as UI rather than as a fight turning; building the marker and rear-arc systems to deliver Conflagration properly was rejected because it reopens a scope that was deliberately bounded to three items. Escalation using mechanics the engine already has delivers the missing experience now and leaves the authored package intact for when its dependencies land. The dual condition is kept as authored, because a Health-only trigger never fires against a slow deck and a Round-only trigger never rewards a fast one.

The reveal reuses `ProgramStrip` and `PhaseBanner` at the Round boundary rather than introducing a component or a modal, consistent with `CONTEXT.md`: the next phase's exact Program is revealed before its first Instant Row, and a trigger reached during a player window takes effect after the Round finishes. Specifically it rides the staggered-playout director in `web/src/store/playout.ts`, which already replays a resolved batch one Boss Beat at a time — announcing each Beat, lighting its chip in `ProgramStrip`, and pausing on a Continue prompt — and which already holds the outcome presentation until its batch finishes replaying. The Phase Reveal is another moment in that sequence, not a second pacing mechanism beside it; the Minion intent resolving after the Slow Window rides it for the same reason, because a Minion acting silently while every Boss Beat gets its own moment would read as a defect. Note that the director is presentation only — nothing it holds is read back into the rules — so none of this touches determinism or replay.

Recording the deferral here matters more than recording the trigger — without it, the Phase Trigger quietly becomes a door back into Engineering ranks 4 and 5.
