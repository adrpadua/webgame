# Resolve Forced Movement one hex at a time

A Card may declare exactly one of `push_tiles` or `pull_tiles`. A positive value requires `target_type: "piece"` and `range_tiles >= 1`; the Content Catalog rejects invalid combinations at load and names the authored file. Charge Modifiers cannot alter Forced Movement. This keeps the first supported family deliberately narrow: content chooses direction and distance, while the engine owns geometry and consequences.

Forced Movement resolves as its own generated `displace_piece` action after the Card's damage actions. That ordering is load-bearing. Damage that defeats a Minion removes it before displacement, so the follow-up records an unavailable target and never moves a defeated piece. A pure displacement Card still produces the firing Hero's strike presentation before the target moves.

Movement is resolved against the live rules board one hex at a time. On each step, Pull snaps the target-to-source vector to the nearest legal hex edge; Push snaps the source-to-target vector and continues along that edge from the target. The engine recomputes the snap after every successful step, with the existing facing order as the deterministic tie-break. A nonexistent off-board hex stops with `edge`; an occupied hex stops with `occupied`; consuming every requested step stops with `complete`. Partial and fully blocked displacement are successful outcomes, including `actual_distance: 0`, because the Card legally resolved even when the board prevented motion.

Forced Movement changes coordinates but not facing. It ignores `blocks_voluntary_movement`, because that Hazard rule names voluntary movement specifically, but every Hazard on every entered hex still generates its enter damage in path order. As with `move_hero`, coordinate movement is the parent action and Hazard damage is its generated consequence: the displacement finishes and records one fact before those damage actions resolve. Occupancy always blocks, including Heroes, Minions, and the Boss. The Guarded Front grants no movement immunity: it is a positional condition whose benefit is lost if a future effect legally moves its occupant.

The displacement action's Resolution Fact is the presentation and replay authority. It records `from`, `to`, `requested_distance`, `actual_distance`, and `stop_reason`; the board derives the target's `move` effect from those coordinates rather than comparing snapshots or recomputing geometry. This preserves ADR 0017's single rules-board authority and ADR 0019's rule that presentation animates facts rather than inventing outcomes.
