The one padding rule
padding-inline: calc(var(--wb-inset) + var(--wb-gutter));
--wb-inset is how deep the cut reaches this plate's content. The leading edge is furthest in at the top and reaches zero at the bottom; the trailing edge does the reverse. So content spanning the plate meets the full offset, and content held to the middle band meets half of it (wb-plate-centered). --wb-gutter is the breathing room past the cut, and it is the only figure in the system tuned by eye.
Nothing else sets padding-inline. A plate that wants width back lowers its gutter or declares where its content sits — it never ducks under its own cut. One exception, wb-gutter-raked, is for a plate carrying a text column: a raked box read as a column is a wedge, so it measures each side at the corner that side is worst at and adds the notch back on top.
Its three gutters are variables, not constants. The measurement generalises to any raked column; the numbers were tuned on a ~374pt dock card. A 138pt party frame taking them unchanged spends a quarter of its width on padding and truncates an ally's name at six characters — so p-raked-narrow keeps the measurement and cuts the gutters to what the width affords (10/9 rather than 18/15 at that size). The principle applies at every width; the constants do not.
A browser check holds every visible plate to padding ≥ --wb-inset, so a new size cannot ship with content sitting in its own cut. This rule replaced seven hand-set values, a per-caller utility, and two inline styles no class could beat.
