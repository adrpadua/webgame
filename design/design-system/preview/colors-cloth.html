The gold-ringed step is the interface direction's own hex; the rest are what that material looks like lit, dim, or as a dark ground. A material picks its step from the ground it sits on — the ramp exists so it can, not so a designer can pick a nicer blue.
The Role assignment
--cloth-500TankThe deepest step — the Role that stands in front.
--cloth-400DamageTwo Damage seats share this step. They are one Role, and the channel names Roles.
--cloth-300HealerThe lit step, where the Role that answers damage is easiest to find in a column.
This ramp used to be described as per-Hero, with these six steps standing in for Shield Wall's. The party frames settled it the other way: a frame wears its Role's step, read off the Keyword its seat's whole deck agrees on. Per-Hero could not survive a column — two Heroes of the same Role in two different blues says they differ in something, and they do not.
Role is the quietest thing a frame can say, and it yields. Living gold takes the accent for the one frame the player can operate, ember for a body on the floor, ember coral for the Boss's attention — so cloth is what shows when nothing is happening to that Hero, which is most of the time.
